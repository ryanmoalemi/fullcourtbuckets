## What Smesko and Reese said after Game 5

Dream coach Karl Smesko and Angel Reese criticized the officiating after Game 5. On the [Dream's site](https://dream.wnba.com/news/dream-faces-must-win-game-in-atlanta-after-game-5-loss-to-valkyries), Smesko called the contact allowed on Reese "ridiculous," and he said officials missed double-dribbles and travels. Reese said a change in the officiating happened in Game 4.

Smesko, quoted on that page: "We're missing shots that we should make, but that (the officiating) needs to be a whole lot better than it was." He also said: "And it's time that if you push somebody with two hands as they drive, it's got to be a foul. And I'm kind of tired of some of the excuses that I'm hearing."

At the postgame press conference, [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/wnba/news/angel-reese-karl-smesko-blast-officiating-wnba-finals/) reported this from Smesko: "The contact they allow on Angel compared to everybody else is just ridiculous. Just ridiculous. We should have gone to the foul line six-to-eight more times just on that." The same remarks are in this [UnderdogWNBA clip](https://x.com/UnderdogWNBA/status/2114916125074940127) from the press conference.

Reese said at that press conference, again per CBS Sports: "It definitely shifted when we went up 2-1." She said she did not know what happened, but that it took a big turn. On a Game 4 possession she said [Gabby Williams](/wnba/gabby-williams/) "pulled me on purpose" and was screaming "Foul, foul, foul" with no call. She said the officials answered, "Oh, we'll get you next time." The [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/dream-facing-elimination-wnba-finals-9ce9d86729283a4d84e21a740cd65a93) ran its own account of the Dream after the loss.

## Where the whistle changed and where it did not

The jump is in offensive fouls. The Dream had 2 in Games 1 through 3 and 9 in Games 4 and 5. The Valkyries had 2, then 4.

Game 4 did not take defensive whistles away from Atlanta. The Valkyries committed 26 personal fouls, the most in the series, and 14 shooting fouls, tying Game 3. The Dream shot 31 free throws in Game 4. Their series high is 35, in Game 2.

Game 5 is where Atlanta's free throw trips fell. The Dream had 14 attempts, and the Valkyries committed 9 shooting fouls. Both are that team's fewest in the series. Add the five games and the Dream still lead at the line, 130 attempts to 78. Atlanta made 101. Golden State made 59.

The same count on the five Dream playoff games before the Finals is 8 offensive fouls: 2 and 4 against Washington, then 0, 1 and 1 against New York. Reese had 3 (1, 0, 0, 1, 1). Play-by-play: [Washington Game 1](https://www.wnba.com/game/was-vs-atl-1042600131/play-by-play), [Washington Game 2](https://www.wnba.com/game/atl-vs-was-1042600132/play-by-play), [New York Game 1](https://www.wnba.com/game/nyl-vs-atl-1042600201/play-by-play), [New York Game 2](https://www.wnba.com/game/nyl-vs-atl-1042600202/play-by-play), [New York Game 3](https://www.wnba.com/game/atl-vs-nyl-1042600203/play-by-play). Nine offensive fouls in Finals Games 4 and 5 are more than those five games combined. That is a count of calls, not a rate.
