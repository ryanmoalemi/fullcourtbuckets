## What she told Lithuanian outlets

The English below is a Full Court Buckets translation of the Lithuanian. It is not a transcript of Jocyte speaking English.

BasketNews published the interview on Nov. 8, 2026. The headline is her line, "Nelabai noriu grįžti į tą komandą," which means she does not really want to go back to that team. The part of the interview that loads without a subscription says she took the title in a very mixed way. She said she had already said, at the end-of-season press conference, that she sort of understood why she did not play, and that the feeling was ambiguous. It was nice for the team, she said, and she did not really contribute. She said she did not even know her own position on whether she would want to go back next year. The free text stops mid-sentence, after she says she still has not fully understood why she did not play, "and those excuses..." The rest is behind BasketNews's BN+ paywall. This page did not open it.

LRT published a separate television interview on Nov. 9. The interviewer said Jocyte had already mentioned that she would not really want to return. Her answer:

"I don't know a person who would want to come back with a smile after a season like this. If the situation had been different, if they had given me a chance and let me play, then maybe they would have seen that I was not really ready. But now they did not try at all and did not let me show what I can do, and they did not explain why. That is why I don't really want to go back."

She said the staff talked at first about physicality, adjusting, and learning the plays, and that she was left without the real reason. "The idea of physicality is very broad. I don't know if they wanted me to become the most physical player in a week."

On changing teams, she told LRT a trade is possible if the two clubs agree, and that a new team arriving next year is another option. She said she is not in a hurry, and that she is not in a hurry to return to America because it is not her dream country. She did not name general manager Ohemaa Nyanin in the LRT interview this page read.

[Kenzo Fukuda's Nov. 9 post](https://x.com/kenzofuku/status/2119888523537072256) is the note English-language stories are built on. He wrote that a lot can be lost in a machine translation, and that Jocyte had told Nyanin and the front office she does not want to return. His [Nov. 10 ClutchPoints story](https://clutchpoints.com/wnba/golden-state-valkyries/valkyries-news-juste-jocytes-future-golden-state-uncertain-candid-comments-lithuanian-media) says the translation of the full BasketNews interview was checked by a Lithuanian speaker, and it quotes her describing a conversation with the general manager. Those sentences are in the paywalled portion. This page does not repeat them as verified quotes.

[Sporting News](https://www.sportingnews.com/us/wnba/news/why-valkyries-former-top-five-pick-reportedly-wants-leave-wnba-champions/fc51d4b1a028f5698692c710) wrote that she asked Nyanin to facilitate a trade. [Yardbarker](https://www.yardbarker.com/wnba/articles/valkyries_juste_jocyte_wants_out_even_after_wnba_title_win/s1_127_44539906), reprinting Larry Brown Sports, and [EssentiallySports](https://www.essentiallysports.com/wnba-basketball-news-dont-really-want-to-go-back-valkyries-player-casts-doubt-on-golden-state-future-while-voicing-frustration-with-natalie-nakase/) also describe a trade request. The LRT answer this page translated says a trade is possible and that she is not in a hurry. It does not say she has asked the team to trade her.

A search of the Valkyries site on Nov. 10 did not turn up a response. The team's [offseason tracker](https://valkyries.wnba.com/news/valkyries-2026-offseason-tracker), updated April 18, records the April 12 activation of her rookie scale contract and does not mention the November interviews. ClutchPoints wrote that the team had not immediately answered a request for comment. No statement from the team or from Jocyte was on the pages checked for this story.

## What the rules say about next season

She is not a free agent on the strength of one season played. The Valkyries' April 12 note says they activated her rookie scale contract. [Article V, Section 4(a)](https://www.wnbpa.com/_files/ugd/8121a3_675911316ca142178932adc1e56519ac.pdf) of the signed agreement says a draft rookie's scale contract covers three seasons and gives the team an option for a fourth, which the team may exercise by May 1 after the second season. Section 4(c) says a scale contract signed after the first regular season following the draft uses the salary table for the same draft slot in the draft just before the first season the contract covers. This page does not have her contract, so it does not print a dollar figure.

Our [2027 free-agent file](/news/wnba-free-agents-2027/) lists her as reserved, with one season on record. That label is the class if a contract ends. It is not a finding that her deal ends after 2026. [Head coach Natalie Nakase](/news/wnba-head-coaches-2027/) is still the coach in that coaching file.

[Article XIV, Section 9(c)](https://www.wnbpa.com/_files/ugd/8121a3_675911316ca142178932adc1e56519ac.pdf) says the prioritization suspension does not apply to a player with zero, one or two years of service at the end of the previous season. A year of service, in the agreement's definition, is a season on a standard contract in which she was on the active list for any part of the regular season. She was on the active list in 2026. The 2027 prioritization suspension in that section does not apply to her on that count. LRT has her planning an offseason league, Project B, and saying she is not rushing back to the United States. She did not say in that interview that she will skip the 2027 WNBA season.

[Article XIV, Section 1](https://www.wnbpa.com/_files/ugd/8121a3_675911316ca142178932adc1e56519ac.pdf) says a player who publicly expresses a desire to be traded is in violation of that section. The LRT answer describes a trade as possible and says she is not in a hurry. This page does not decide whether that answer is the request the section names.

She told LRT a new team is coming next year. The [April 9, 2026 Board of Governors release](https://www.wnba.com/news/board-of-governors-approve-wnba-expansion-teams) says Cleveland begins play in 2028, Detroit in 2029 and Philadelphia in 2030. The [2026 expansion draft FAQ](https://www.wnba.com/news/2026-expansion-draft-faq) said each existing team could protect up to five players. That draft has already been held. This page did not find a published protection number for 2028.

The seven top-5 picks with no line in the minutes file are Jayne Appel (2010, No. 5), Evelyn Akhator (2017, No. 3), Nia Coffey (2017, No. 5), Lauren Cox (2020, No. 3), Kysre Gondrezick (2021, No. 4), Stephanie Soares (2023, No. 4) and Lou Lopez Sénéchal (2023, No. 5). A lower first season for any of them would change the rank. Their names and the draft-page links are in the second CSV.
