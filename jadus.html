A smaller ADU option

What is a JADU?

A JADU is a compact residential unit contained within an existing or proposed single-family home. It offers more independence than a standard bedroom without requiring a new detached backyard building.

In San Diego, a JADU may be created within the primary residence or an attached garage. It must include a separate exterior entrance and an efficiency kitchen. The unit may have its own bathroom or share sanitation facilities with the primary home.

JADUs are especially useful for aging parents, adult children, caregivers, guests or long-term tenants when homeowners want to use existing square footage efficiently.

2026 planning note: JADU rules have changed in recent years, including owner-occupancy treatment when the unit has separate sanitation facilities. Final requirements should be verified for the property's jurisdiction and permit date. See our San Diego ADU rules page for current local requirements, or the ADU handbook for a full planning overview.

Smart use of existing space

When a JADU makes more sense than a full ADU

A JADU is often the strongest option when the property has limited yard area, the owner does not need a large rental unit, or an existing part of the house can be converted without compromising the main floor plan.

  • Less new foundation, framing and roofing than new construction
  • Potentially shorter utility routes inside the existing home
  • Preserves the backyard for outdoor living or a separate ADU
  • Keeps family members close while creating daily independence
  • Can be paired with an ADU on an eligible single-family lot
  • May be easier to heat, cool and maintain due to its compact size

Property screening

Is your San Diego home a good JADU candidate?

The best JADU projects start with space that already has a logical exterior entrance, natural light and a practical route to plumbing and electrical systems. The conversion should also leave the primary home functional after the space is separated.

01

An underused bedroom near an exterior wall

A bedroom with an existing exterior door or a practical location for a new entrance can reduce circulation conflicts inside the home.

02

An attached garage with suitable dimensions

Garages can offer a defined footprint, but the slab, ceiling, waterproofing, fire separation, natural light and utility conditions need careful review.

03

A large suite near existing plumbing

A bathroom or laundry wall near the proposed kitchenette may reduce plumbing work, provided the existing systems have adequate capacity.

04

A floor plan with two distinct living zones

Homes with a rear wing, side suite or separated hallway often adapt more naturally than homes where every route passes through the main living room.

San Diego JADU requirements

Important rules to understand before design begins

The table below summarizes major City of San Diego planning points. Incorporated cities and unincorporated San Diego County may use different forms, procedures and local standards.

Planning item General JADU standard Design implication
Location Contained within an existing or proposed single-family residence, including qualifying space in an attached garage. A detached garage or freestanding backyard building is generally evaluated as an ADU, not a JADU.
Floor area The City of San Diego identifies a minimum of 150 square feet and a maximum of 500 square feet. Every wall, cabinet, appliance and circulation path must be planned carefully in a compact footprint.
Exterior entrance A separate exterior entry is required from the primary dwelling. The entrance should also provide privacy, lighting, address visibility and safe access.
Kitchen A cooking facility with appliances, a reasonable food-preparation counter and reasonable storage cabinets. The kitchenette must be designed as a legal cooking area, not merely a wet bar or temporary appliance station.
Bathroom A JADU may have separate sanitation facilities or share sanitation facilities with the main residence. A private bathroom improves independence but takes valuable floor area and adds plumbing and ventilation work.
Owner occupancy Under current state law, owner occupancy is generally tied to a JADU that shares sanitation facilities with the primary residence. Confirm the current deed-restriction and owner-occupancy language before making rental or financing assumptions.
Parking Additional parking is generally not required solely for a JADU. Converting an attached garage may still affect practical household parking and storage.
Separate sale A JADU is not ordinarily sold separately from the primary residence. Treat it as part of the same real property when evaluating long-term use.
Rental duration Current state law requires a JADU rental term to be longer than 30 days. A JADU should be planned for family occupancy or longer-term housing, not nightly vacation rental use.
Impact fees A compliant JADU of 500 square feet or less is protected from specified impact fees under current state law. Building permit, plan review and project-specific costs may still apply.

This content is a planning overview, not a permit determination. Coastal, historic, fire-hazard, environmentally sensitive and other property conditions can add review requirements.

JADU or standard ADU?

Choose the unit type around your actual goal.

Junior ADU

Best for compact living inside a single-family home

  • Maximum 500-square-foot format
  • Uses space within the home or attached garage
  • Efficiency kitchen rather than a large full kitchen
  • Private or shared sanitation may be possible
  • Strong fit for relatives, caregivers or one occupant
  • Preserves yard space for other uses

Standard ADU

Best when greater independence and space are priorities

  • May be attached, detached or created through garage conversion
  • Can support larger kitchens and multiple bedrooms
  • Includes its own sanitation facilities
  • May offer stronger rental flexibility and separation
  • Usually requires a larger project budget
  • Can involve more extensive site and utility work

Designing 500 square feet well

A JADU should feel compact, not improvised.

Successful small-space design depends on clear circulation, correctly sized furniture, useful storage and separation between sleeping, cooking and entry areas. A poorly positioned bathroom door or oversized cabinet can consume a surprising amount of usable space.

  • Use built-in storage where freestanding furniture would block circulation
  • Place plumbing fixtures near viable drain and water routes
  • Protect daylight when adding interior separation walls
  • Use durable flooring across the compact open-plan area
  • Provide a practical location for clothes, cleaning supplies and luggage
  • Coordinate appliance clearances before ordering cabinets
  • Create sound separation from bedrooms and living rooms in the main home

Systems and construction

What usually needs to change during a JADU conversion

A

Exterior entrance and separation

The design may require a new exterior door, landing, path, lighting, weather protection, locks and interior wall or door modifications.

B

Kitchen electrical and plumbing

Cooking appliances, refrigeration, receptacles, lighting, sink plumbing, ventilation and cabinetry must be coordinated within a narrow kitchen zone.

C

Bathroom work

A private bathroom may require drain connections, venting, supply lines, waterproofing, exhaust and enough space around every fixture.

D

Heating, cooling and ventilation

The unit needs a compliant comfort and ventilation strategy that accounts for the smaller enclosed floor area and available equipment locations.

E

Garage upgrades

Garage projects often need slab evaluation, moisture protection, insulation, wall and ceiling upgrades, window changes and removal or modification of the vehicle door.

F

Life safety

Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, emergency escape, safe electrical work, fire-resistance details and permitted egress must be addressed in the plans.

Private or shared bathroom

One bathroom decision changes the entire project.

Sharing the primary home's bathroom can preserve more JADU living space and reduce plumbing work. A private bathroom creates a more independent unit but can consume roughly a fifth or more of a very compact floor plan once walls, fixtures and circulation are included.

The decision may also affect owner-occupancy treatment under current state law. It should be made after reviewing the intended occupant, rental plans, available plumbing, privacy and long-term property goals.

Budget planning

What determines the cost of a JADU in San Diego?

JADUs use existing space, but they are still permitted residential construction projects. Cost is driven by the condition of that space and how many residential systems must be added or relocated.

Existing bedroom conversion

Often the least structurally intensive option when the room already has finished walls, compliant windows, insulation and nearby plumbing. Kitchen, entrance, separation and electrical work remain important.

Attached garage conversion

May require substantial enclosure and building-envelope work. Slab conditions, floor level, garage-door replacement, fire separation, moisture and ceiling height can materially affect cost.

JADU with a private bathroom

Adds plumbing, waterproofing, tile, fixtures, exhaust and finish work. Cost rises further when the sewer connection is distant or below-slab work is required.

JADU within a larger remodel

Combining the JADU with a kitchen renovation, addition or whole-home remodel can improve the final layout but requires a broader plan set and more coordination.

Common cost drivers

  • New exterior door, landing, walkway and lighting
  • Kitchen cabinets, countertop, sink and appliances
  • Electrical panel capacity and new circuits
  • Private bathroom and sewer routing
  • Heating, cooling and ventilation equipment
  • Garage slab, moisture and insulation corrections
  • Fire and sound separation from the primary home
  • Window, egress and natural-light changes
  • Relocation of laundry or household storage
  • Plans, engineering, energy documents and permits

Multigenerational living

A private space can keep family closer.

A well-designed JADU can give a parent, adult child or caregiver a secure entrance, personal kitchen area and defined living space while maintaining immediate access to the household when help is needed.

Frequently asked questions

San Diego JADU FAQs

What does JADU stand for?

JADU stands for Junior Accessory Dwelling Unit. It is a compact unit of no more than 500 square feet that is created within an existing or proposed single-family residence.

Can a JADU be built in a detached garage?

A JADU must be contained within the qualifying single-family residence. The City of San Diego identifies an attached garage as a possible location. A detached garage conversion is generally treated as an ADU rather than a JADU.

Does a JADU need its own bathroom?

No. A JADU may include separate sanitation facilities or share sanitation facilities with the primary residence. A private bathroom generally provides greater independence but uses valuable floor area.

Does a JADU need a kitchen?

Yes. It must provide an efficiency kitchen that includes a cooking facility with appliances, a food-preparation counter and storage cabinets reasonably sized for the unit.

Can I rent out a JADU in San Diego?

A permitted JADU may generally be used for longer-term housing, subject to current state law, local regulations and the recorded restrictions for the property. Current state law requires the rental term to be longer than 30 days.

Do I have to live on the property?

Under current California law, owner occupancy is generally required when the JADU shares sanitation facilities with the primary residence. Owner occupancy is not generally required when the JADU has separate sanitation facilities, subject to current law and recorded documents.

Can a JADU have an interior connection to the house?

The permitted configuration should be reviewed for the specific design. The JADU must have its own exterior entrance, while any interior connection must comply with applicable code and separation requirements.

Can I build both a JADU and an ADU?

On an eligible single-family property, California law and City of San Diego rules may allow a JADU together with qualifying ADU development. The exact combination should be verified during feasibility review.

Is a JADU cheaper than a detached ADU?

It is often less expensive because it uses existing space and avoids much of the new foundation, exterior wall and roof construction. Costs can still be significant when converting a garage or adding a private bathroom and major utility work.

How long does a JADU permit take?

Timing depends on the jurisdiction, completeness of the plans and property-specific issues. Current California law includes application processing protections, but corrections and specialized review can extend the overall timeline.
