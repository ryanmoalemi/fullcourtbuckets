    Liberty fire Chris DeMarco: only 3 WNBA coaches have lasted more than two seasons

    By Ryan MoalemiUpdated November 4, 2026

    The New York Liberty will not keep Chris DeMarco. Only 3 of 15 WNBA head coaches have lasted more than two seasons with their current team. Cheryl Reeve has 17 with the Lynx. The six coaches with two seasons and the four with one have 16 seasons combined. Open-job day counts are in the 2027 WNBA coaching tracker.

    Why New York moved on

    On Nov. 3, 2026, the Liberty account posted "Thank you, Coach DeMarco." The graphic reads "THANK YOU" and "CHRIS DEMARCO." The same morning, Alexa Philippou of ESPN reported that sources said the team will not retain him. That story's publish time is 1:03 p.m. UTC, which is 5:03 a.m. Pacific. Philippou also reported that after the semifinal sweep, DeMarco said he expected to return in 2027. The WNBA.com team page lists no Liberty head coach.

    The hiring release, published Dec. 3, 2025, says the Liberty hired DeMarco, a Golden State Warriors assistant, that day. General manager Jonathan Kolb is the person quoted.

    In 2026 the Liberty went 26-18 and were the No. 8 seed. On June 30 they beat Las Vegas 93-85 in the Commissioner's Cup final at Barclays Center, on the WNBA.com game page. They then beat the No. 1 Lynx 2-0, in our recaps of Game 1 and Game 2. Atlanta, the No. 4 seed, swept the semifinals, 92-82, 101-98 in overtime and 85-83, in our recaps of Game 1, Game 2 and Game 3.

    The WNBA named Cheryl Reeve Coach of the Year on Sept. 28, her fifth time, with 40 of 74 votes, after a 33-11 season. The release puts her career regular-season wins at 397, the win total in the Lynx row above, and says the Lynx trailed New York 1-0 in that first-round series. New York finished the sweep in Game 2. What the roster does next, including Breanna Stewart, Sabrina Ionescu and Jonquel Jones, is in five moves after the sweep. Service time is in the 2027 free-agent file.

    Both Finals coaches were in their second season

    Natalie Nakase and Karl Smesko were hired 34 days apart, on Oct. 10 and Nov. 13, 2024. Both coached in the 2026 Finals in their second season. Nakase's Valkyries won the series. The WNBA.com recap of Game 7 says Golden State beat Atlanta 68-63. Our recap is Game 7.

    Smesko's two seasons sum to 60-28. Atlanta's 2026 line on the standings is 30-14, so 2025 was 30-14 too. Nakase's two seasons sum to 55-33. Golden State was 32-12 in 2026, so 2025 was 23-21. Down 2-1 in the Finals, Nakase started Tiffany Hayes and Kaila Charles in Game 4, the San Francisco Chronicle reported. Hayes scored 11 points in Game 7 on the WNBA.com box score.

    Eight hires had a first season in 2025: Nakase (Oct. 10, 2024), White (Nov. 1), Marsh (Nov. 3), Smesko (Nov. 13), Roberts (Nov. 19), Meziane (Dec. 4), Johnson (Dec. 23) and Chris Koclanes in Dallas (Dec. 23, 2024). Koclanes was not the 2026 coach. Fernandez's Oct. 27, 2025 release names him as the next Wings coach.

    Leave out Golden State, Portland and Toronto, the expansion teams whose current coach is the first, and leave out the two vacancies. Seven of those 10 jobs are in a first or second season.

    Connecticut's row is 22-66. The Sun were 11-33 in 2026, so 2025 was 11-33 too. Morgan Tuck told USA TODAY Sports on Oct. 30 that the plan is to keep Meziane when the franchise plays in Houston in 2027. "Our plan is to keep Rachid and that he'll be with us next year," she said. The table still lists the Connecticut Sun, the name on the WNBA.com team page on Nov. 4.

    Hires whose first season was 2026

    Five of those hires have a date on a release: Alex Sarama (Oct. 17, 2025), Fernandez (Oct. 27, 2025), Sonia Raman (Oct. 28, 2025), Sandy Brondello (Nov. 4, 2025) and DeMarco (Dec. 3, 2025). The Fire release is dated Oct. 17, 2025 on the team's news list. DeMarco's job is the one that opened on Nov. 3.

    Los Angeles

    The Sparks parted ways with Lynne Roberts on Sept. 25, 2026, effective that day. Special advisor Farhan Zaidi's quote on the release thanks her through "our last game last night." The hiring story, dated Nov. 20, 2024, says the team announced it Tuesday, which was Nov. 19. Her two seasons were 21-23 in 2025 and 16-28 in 2026, 37-51 (.420). Both seasons were outside the eight-team playoff field. The 2026 Sparks were 16-28, 11th on the standings.
