    Diana Taurasi headlines Women's Basketball Hall of Fame Class of 2027

    By Ryan MoalemiNovember 6, 2026

    The Women's Basketball Hall of Fame named Diana Taurasi to the Class of 2027 on Thursday, Nov. 5. She is the ninth person from UConn on the school's list. Induction is Saturday, June 26, 2027, at the Tennessee Theatre in Knoxville.

    The Nov. 5 release gives her UConn career as 2,156 points, 10th in the program, 648 assists, third, and 318 made threes, third. She played from 2000 to 2004. The NCAA titles on that release, and on the Hall page, are 2002, 2003 and 2004.

    The June 15, 2025 release gives Sue Bird 1,378 points and 585 assists at UConn. The other seven rows do not have a college line in this file. A blank in the CSV is not a zero.

    The statement, and two spellings

    Michelle Marciniak, the Hall's CEO, in a statement USA TODAY published Nov. 5, 2026: "For nearly thirty years, the Women's Basketball Hall of Fame has honored the greatest of the game, and we are thrilled to welcome these six inductees into the distinguished seats of the Hall of Fame. Selection for this esteemed recognition celebrates the hard work, success, and record-breaking journeys for those to join this global class. We cannot wait to welcome the Class of 2027."

    Sportsnet carried the same statement that day and spells it honoured. The induction page, checked Nov. 6, lists the six and the June 26 date. It does not include the sentence.

    The Hall page spells the building Tennessee Theatre. The UConn release spells it Tennessee Theater. USA TODAY calls June 26, 2027, a Saturday. The Hall page gives the date and does not give the weekday.

    Where the write-ups disagree

    The UConn release says Taurasi was a 10-time All-WNBA first-team selection and a four-time second-team pick, and an 11-time All-Star. The Hall page says 11 All-Star teams and does not list All-WNBA. USA TODAY says 14 All-WNBA selections and does not split first and second team. Ten plus four is 14. This page uses the UConn split. A 2021 WNBA.com feature lists the same 10 first-team years and says the 2021 team was not out yet. It also gives her 9,174 points, so it is a 2021 snapshot.

    USA TODAY says she led the league in scoring five times, and that she is the league's all-time leader in 3-pointers made and in free throws made. The Hall page and the UConn release do not say that. The 2021 feature lists five scoring titles, in 2006 and from 2008 through 2011, and lists her first in threes and free throws at that feature's totals. This page does not use five, and it does not use those leader lines.

    The UConn release says she is the only player with more than 10,000 points, the only six-time Olympic gold medalist in basketball, a seven-time Russian National League champion, and a four-time USA Basketball Female Athlete of the Year. It also says she has three World Championship gold medals and one bronze. The Hall page names the gold years, 2010, 2014 and 2018, and the Olympic years, 2004, 2008, 2012, 2016, 2020 and 2024. It does not mention a bronze or the Russian league. The EuroLeague years on the Hall page are 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010, 2013 and 2016.

    Sportsnet says Brondello's Opals won World Cup silver in 2018 and in 2022. The Hall page says silver in 2018 and bronze in 2022, plus Olympic bronze in 2024 and Asia Cup gold in 2025. Basketball Australia's Oct. 21, 2026 story also says bronze in 2022. This page uses the Hall page.

    That Basketball Australia story says Brondello was named to the Sport Australia Hall of Fame Class of 2026, with the gala on Nov. 24, 2026, at Centrepiece, Melbourne Park. The Sport Australia member page says she was inducted in 2026. Sportsnet's Oct. 20 story says the Tempo announced the selection that day. This page does not pick one announcement date.

    USA TODAY says Holland was UCLA's director of women's athletics from 1975 to 1981. The Hall page says first full-time director from 1975 to 1980, then senior associate athletic director from 1980 to 1996. This page uses the Hall page. USA TODAY also says Ann Meyers led UCLA to a pair of national titles. The Hall page says Holland oversaw UCLA's AIAW national title in 1978, the first won by a major university. It does not say a pair.

    USA TODAY calls Bolin Kazmer "Machine Gun Molly." The Hall page uses Molly Bolin Kazmer and does not use the nickname. The same Hall page says she averaged 54.8 points a game in a season at Moravia High School in Iowa, scored 83 in a game there, and was WBL co-MVP with Ann Meyers in 1980.

    The Hall page says Kantner and Violet Palmer were the first two women hired as NBA referees, for 1997-2002, and that ESPN hired Kantner as a women's college basketball rules analyst in 2025. USA TODAY adds 247 NBA games, 11 years as WNBA supervisor, and world championships in 1994 and 1998. Those three are not on the Hall page. The supervisor years on the Hall page are 1997-1998 and 2005-2016.

    USA TODAY says Thibault was a U.S. women's assistant in 2008 and again in 2024, and that Belgium reached the 2026 FIBA World Cup quarterfinals. The Hall page says he was a U.S. assistant for the 2008 Olympic gold, and that he led Belgium to the 2025 EuroBasket title. It does not mention 2024 or a 2026 quarterfinal.

    NBC Connecticut, Jennifer Lambert, Nov. 5, repeats the UConn figures for 2,156 points, 648 assists and 318 threes, the three NCAA titles, the three WNBA titles, the two Finals MVPs, the six Olympic golds, and the eight earlier UConn names.
