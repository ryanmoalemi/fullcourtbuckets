WNBA Expansion Teams 2026: Portland Fire and Toronto Tempo

How the Portland Fire and Toronto Tempo were built, how 2026 went, and which WNBA teams come next.

Last checked Sep 29, 2026

The WNBA has 15 teams in 2026. The two new ones are the Portland Fire and the Toronto Tempo, and both played their first games in May 2026. Toronto finished 11-33 and Portland went 17-27, and neither team made the playoffs.

Portland Fire: the name came back

The Fire are Portland's second WNBA team, and they took the name of the first one. The league awarded Portland its 15th franchise on Sept. 18, 2024, with RAJ Sports, led by Lisa Bhathal Merage and Alex Bhathal, as owner. The Bhathal family also owns the NWSL's Portland Thorns. Home games are at Moda Center downtown.

The WNBA called it a return to Portland "after more than 20 years." The original Portland Fire played three seasons, from 2000 to 2002. Linda Hargrove coached all three. They went 10-22, then 11-21, then 16-16, and never reached the playoffs. Jackie Stiles won Rookie of the Year for the Fire in 2001.

The new front office is pretty much built from scratch. The Fire hired Alex Sarama, a Cleveland Cavaliers assistant, as their first head coach in October 2025. He's known for a training method called the Constraints-Led Approach, and he told ESPN an expansion team was an ideal place to use it because nobody had to unlearn an old system. Vanja Černivec runs the roster as general manager.

Portland fans showed up right away. The Fire's May 9 home game against the Chicago Sky drew 19,335 at Moda Center, even though Chicago won 98-83.

Toronto Tempo: the first WNBA team outside the US

Toronto is the first WNBA team based outside the United States. The league made it official on May 23, 2024, naming Toronto its 14th franchise. Kilmer Sports Ventures owns the team. Its chairman, Larry Tanenbaum, is also chairman of Maple Leaf Sports & Entertainment, which owns the Raptors and Maple Leafs.

The Tempo play at Coca-Cola Coliseum at Exhibition Place. When the team was announced, the league said some games might also be played at Scotiabank Arena and in other parts of Canada.

Monica Wright Rogers was hired as the first general manager in February 2025. Sandy Brondello, who won a title with the Phoenix Mercury and later coached the New York Liberty, became head coach in November 2025.

The first game was a close loss. The Washington Mystics beat the Toronto Tempo 68-65 on May 8 in front of 8,210 fans at Coca-Cola Coliseum. The highlight of the season came on June 25, when Marina Mabrey scored 53 points in a win over the Los Angeles Sparks. That tied the WNBA single-game scoring record.

WNBA expansion draft results

The expansion draft was held April 3, 2026. The Fire and Tempo took 22 players between them, and Portland picked first.

WNBA expansion draft rules

The 13 existing teams could each protect up to five players. Everybody else on their rosters was fair game. Here's how it worked:

  • Two rounds. Portland and Toronto alternated picks, and in each round they could take one player from each team's unprotected list.
  • If a picked player didn't have a 2026 contract, her new team got the same rights her old team had.
  • Each expansion team could take only one "potential unrestricted free agent," meaning a player with five or more years of service whose contract had run out.
  • Toronto won a coin toss on March 27 but didn't take the No. 1 expansion pick. It chose the No. 6 pick in the regular draft, which gave Portland the top expansion pick and No. 7 in the regular draft.
  • Both teams made separate trades with the Chicago Sky and agreed not to pick anyone from Chicago's unprotected list.

Who each team took

Portland made Bridget Carleton the No. 1 pick. She'd been a bench player for the Minnesota Lynx, and ESPN reported the Fire didn't want to give Toronto a shot at the Ontario native. Both teams leaned toward veteran role players.

First-season results

Portland had the better year. The Fire finished 17-27, fifth in the West. Toronto went 11-33, sixth in the East. Both teams missed the playoffs.

Golden State made the playoffs as a first-year team (ESPN). Neither 2026 team did. Toronto won just four road games all year. Portland was outscored by 211 points over the season, a lot less than Toronto's 383.

What's next: new WNBA teams coming

Three more teams are confirmed, which will take the league to 18.

  • Cleveland WNBA team: starts in 2028. WNBA.com's site menu already lists it as the Cleveland Sirens.
  • Detroit WNBA team: starts in 2029.
  • Philadelphia WNBA team: starts in 2030.

The WNBA announced these three on June 30, 2025, and the WNBA and NBA Boards of Governors formally approved them on April 9, 2026. All three have NBA ownership groups, and each paid a $250 million expansion fee, per AP. Any other city you see mentioned is not confirmed.

FAQ

How many teams are in the WNBA in 2026?

Fifteen. The Portland Fire and Toronto Tempo joined in 2026, one year after the Golden State Valkyries. Three more teams are already approved after that.

What are the new WNBA teams?

The newest are the Portland Fire and Toronto Tempo, who both started in 2026. Cleveland (2028), Detroit (2029) and Philadelphia (2030) are next.

Are there WNBA expansion teams in 2027?

No team is scheduled to start in 2027. The next confirmed team is Cleveland in 2028.

What were the WNBA expansion draft rules?

Each existing team could protect five players. In each of the two rounds, Portland and Toronto could take one player from each team's unprotected list, and each could take only one potential unrestricted free agent.

Who was the first pick in the 2026 WNBA expansion draft?

Bridget Carleton, taken by Portland from Minnesota. ESPN reported Portland did not want Toronto to have a shot at her.

When does the Detroit WNBA team start?

2029.

When does the Philadelphia WNBA team start?

2030.
