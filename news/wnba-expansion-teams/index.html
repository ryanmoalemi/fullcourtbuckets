    #What the first-season records show

    Most wins is not the best record. Golden State had the most first-season wins, 23, and the first expansion playoff berth. But Detroit's 1998 debut had the higher winning percentage: .567 (17-13) against Golden State's .523 (23-21). Winning percentage adjusts for the 30-game 1998 schedule versus 44 games in 2025; it says nothing about schedule strength, roster rules or how many playoff places there were. Link

    Two of 13 finished above .500: Detroit 1998 and Golden State 2025. One of 13 made the playoffs. Link

    Portland's 17-27 (.386) ranks sixth of 13 and Toronto's 11-33 (.250) ranks ninth. The median first season is 10-22, an exact .3125 (shown .313), the 2000 Portland Fire. Ranks use standard competition ranking, so Orlando and Minnesota (both 15-17 in 1999) tie for third and the next rank is fifth. Link

    This is 13 seasons across 29 years and several sets of roster rules. It describes history; it is not a forecast for Cleveland, Detroit or Philadelphia, and the median is not an expected record.

    Fast Facts for Media & Citations

    • The WNBA has 15 teams in 2026. Of 13 expansion first seasons since 1998, only Golden State (2025) made the playoffs.
    • Detroit 1998 has the best first-season record (.567); Golden State 2025 has the most wins (23). Portland 2026 ranks 6th of 13, Toronto 2026 9th.
    • Toronto won the March 27 coin toss and took the No. 6 pick in the regular draft, which gave Portland the first expansion pick.
    • Cleveland 2028, Detroit 2029, Philadelphia 2030. AP reported a $250 million fee for each.

    #WNBA expansion drafts: rules and results

    The 2026 expansion draft for Portland and Toronto was held April 3, 2026. The Fire and Tempo took 22 players between them, and Portland picked first. The 24 pick slots included two passes, so retention counts below use 22 players, not 24 slots.

    #2026 expansion draft rules (Portland and Toronto)

    Each of the 13 existing teams could protect up to five players from its roster list. Those lists covered players whose rights the teams held, including players on active, suspended, reserved, core or retired lists. Selection could transfer a contract or negotiating rights, not necessarily an immediately available player. Here's how it worked:

    • The draft had two rounds with alternating picks. Portland selected first in round one; Toronto selected first in round two. The official rules governed which unprotected player rights could be acquired from each existing team.
    • If a picked player didn't have a 2026 contract, her new team got the same rights her old team had.
    • Each expansion team could take only one "potential unrestricted free agent," meaning a player with five or more years of service whose contract had run out.
    • After a March 27 coin toss, Toronto chose the sixth pick in the regular draft instead of the No. 1 expansion pick [7]. It chose the No. 6 pick in the regular draft, which gave Portland the top expansion pick and No. 7 in the regular draft.
    • Both teams made separate trades with the Chicago Sky and agreed not to pick anyone from Chicago's unprotected list.

    #What's next: Cleveland, Detroit and Philadelphia

    The WNBA announced the three teams on June 30, 2025, and the WNBA and NBA Boards of Governors formally approved them on April 9, 2026. Cleveland is scheduled to start in 2028, Detroit in 2029 and Philadelphia in 2030. None of them is an active team yet.

    Cleveland's team is the Cleveland Sirens, the WNBA's 16th franchise, which will play at Rocket Arena starting in 2028, according to the Aug. 4, 2026 brand announcement. Detroit's planned home is Little Caesars Arena, per an April 6, 2026 article from Platinum Equity, the firm founded by owner Tom Gores. The league's Philadelphia FAQ says that team will play in South Philadelphia alongside the 76ers; it doesn't name a building for every season, so we don't either.

    #Portland Fire: the name came back

    The Fire are Portland's second WNBA team, and they took the name of the first one. The league awarded Portland its 15th franchise on Sept. 18, 2024, with RAJ Sports, led by Lisa Bhathal Merage and Alex Bhathal, as owner. The Bhathal family also owns the NWSL's Portland Thorns. Home games are at Moda Center downtown.

    The WNBA called it a return to Portland "after more than 20 years." The original Portland Fire played three seasons, from 2000 to 2002. Linda Hargrove coached all three. Their first season ended 10-22, and the team stopped operating after 2002, when the Trail Blazers declined to take over ownership.

    The new front office is pretty much built from scratch. The Fire hired Alex Sarama, a Cleveland Cavaliers assistant, as their first head coach in October 2025. He's known for a training method called the Constraints-Led Approach, and he told ESPN an expansion team was an ideal place to use it because nobody had to unlearn an old system. Vanja Černivec runs the roster as general manager.

    Portland fans showed up right away. The Fire's May 9 home game against the Chicago Sky drew 19,335 at Moda Center, even though Chicago won 98-83. Only 5 of the 91 WNBA Finals games with a recorded attendance since 2002 drew more, according to our Finals attendance database.

    #Toronto Tempo: the first WNBA team outside the US

    Toronto is the first WNBA team based outside the United States. The league made it official on May 23, 2024, naming Toronto its 14th franchise. Kilmer Sports Ventures owns the team. Its chairman, Larry Tanenbaum, is also chairman of Maple Leaf Sports & Entertainment, which owns the Raptors and Maple Leafs.

    The Tempo play at Coca-Cola Coliseum at Exhibition Place. In 2026 some league-designated Toronto home games were played elsewhere in Canada, such as Dallas at Toronto in Montreal on July 10 and Las Vegas at Toronto in Vancouver on Aug. 23. When the team was announced, the league said some games might also be played at Scotiabank Arena and in other parts of Canada.

    Monica Wright Rogers was hired as the first general manager in February 2025. Sandy Brondello, who won a title with the Phoenix Mercury and later coached the New York Liberty, became head coach in November 2025.

    The first game was a close loss. The Washington Mystics beat the Toronto Tempo 68-65 on May 8 in front of 8,210 fans at Coca-Cola Coliseum. The highlight of the season came on June 25, when Marina Mabrey scored 53 points in a win over the Los Angeles Sparks. That tied the WNBA single-game scoring record.

    #Methods, downloads and corrections

    Sample. Every new WNBA franchise's first regular season from 1998 through 2026. The original eight 1997 teams are not expansion teams. Teams announced but not yet playing (Cleveland, Detroit, Philadelphia) are not in the table and have no 0-0 rows.

    Records. 1998, 2006 and 2008 come from WNBA.com standings pages archived by the Internet Archive; 1999 and 2000 come from each team's official season schedule page on WNBA.com, archived, where the running record after the final regular-season game is shown; 2025 comes from the Valkyries' season-finale recap (not the Sept. 4 clinching release, which listed 23-18); 2026 comes from WNBA.com standings. Playoff status for 1998-2000 rests on the Valkyries' release calling them the first expansion team to make the playoffs in an inaugural season.

    Attendance. Default scope is all league-designated regular-season home games, including designated home games at alternate venues (Toronto played some in Montreal and Vancouver in 2026). We publish only figures a league or team release gives with its game count. One crowd or an arena's capacity is never used as a season average. Twelve of 13 rows are not yet compiled, so there is no attendance ranking yet.

    Rounding and ranking. Calculations use exact fractions. Displayed win % is rounded half up to three decimals. Ranks are standard competition ranks over all 13 rows.

    #FAQ

    #How many teams are in the WNBA in 2026?

    Fifteen. The Portland Fire and Toronto Tempo joined in 2026, one year after the Golden State Valkyries. Three more teams were approved on April 9, 2026, for 2028, 2029 and 2030.

    #Are there WNBA expansion teams in 2027?

    No team is scheduled to start in 2027. The next confirmed team is Cleveland in 2028.

    #Has a WNBA expansion team made the playoffs in its first year?

    Once. The 2025 Golden State Valkyries were the first expansion team to make the playoffs in their first season, and lost to Minnesota in the first round. Portland and Toronto did not make the 2026 playoffs.

    #How much is a WNBA expansion fee?

    For Cleveland, Detroit and Philadelphia, AP reported $250 million each. The league's announcement doesn't state a fee.
