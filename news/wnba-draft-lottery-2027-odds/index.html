    Houston's most likely 2027 lottery pick is No. 3

    By Ryan MoalemiNovember 8, 2026

    Houston and Toronto each hold 329 of 1,000 combinations for the No. 1 pick on Sunday, Nov. 15. Houston's most likely result is No. 3, at 38.3 percent. The team with the most combinations won the No. 1 pick in 10 of the 16 lotteries where a WNBA.com page states the answer.

    Chance of each 2027 lottery pick

    Houston's most likely pick is No. 3 (38.3 percent), not No. 1 (32.9 percent). Toronto's most likely pick is No. 1 (32.9 percent). No. 3 is next, at 29.5 percent.

    Houston is one of the two drawn picks 61.7 percent of the time. The other 38.3 percent, Houston is the worst record left and picks third. Toronto is also one of the two drawn picks 61.7 percent of the time. Toronto picks third when Houston is drawn and Toronto is not (29.5 percent). Toronto picks fourth when neither of them is drawn (8.8 percent). In that 8.8 percent, Houston is still third.

    The same arithmetic sets a floor for every row. Washington cannot fall past fifth (2.2 percent there). Seattle cannot land on third. Portland cannot land on third or fourth. Los Angeles's own pick cannot land on third, fourth, or fifth. The Phoenix ticket, if it is not drawn first or second, is seventh every time (97.2 percent).

    The two Los Angeles rows are separate tickets. Added together they are 36 combinations, 3.6 percent, at No. 1. They cannot both be the No. 1 pick.

    Winning percentage on the release is .250 for Houston and for Toronto. The other percentages, from the records in that table, are Chicago .295 (26-62), Seattle .352 (31-57), Portland .386 (17-27), Los Angeles .420 (37-51), and Phoenix .489 (43-45).

    The 10 "yes" years are 2009, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2020, 2021, 2023, 2024, and 2026. The six "no" years are 2005 (Charlotte, 9.7 percent), 2006 (Minnesota, 16.7 percent), 2007 (Phoenix, smallest odds), 2019 (Las Vegas had the second-most, Indiana had 442), 2022 (Washington had the third-most, Indiana had 442), and 2025 (Dallas had 227, Los Angeles had 442).

    The WNBA timeline is the source for 2002 through 2010. It dates the first lottery to Nov. 13, 2001, when Seattle won the 2002 pick, and it does not print Seattle's chances. Cleveland won on April 24, 2003. Phoenix won on Dec. 3, 2003. Charlotte won on Dec. 1, 2004, at 9.7 percent, and the timeline says Charlotte beat the odds. Minnesota won on Oct. 24, 2005, at 16.7 percent. Phoenix won on Oct. 26, 2006, and the timeline says that was the first lottery won by the team with the smallest odds. Minnesota won on Nov. 5, 2009, with 428 of 1,000, New York's 261 plus Minnesota's 167. The page does not say 428 was the most, so 2010 stays out of the 16.

    A Sept. 24, 2015 schedule release looks backward: Phoenix won the lottery in September 2012 for the 2013 draft, Connecticut won the next one and drafted Chiney Ogwumike, and Seattle won the August 2014 lottery and used the pick on Jewell Loyd. That release does not print the 2013, 2014, or 2015 combination counts. The Nov. 23, 2025 results release says the results matched the order of chances ahead of the 2009, 2015, 2016, and 2018 drafts, and that 2026 was the fifth time. That is why 2015 is in the 16 as a yes, with no number in the chances cell.

    The later results pages are 2016 (Seattle, 442, Sept. 24, 2015), 2017 (San Antonio, 442, Sept. 28, 2016), 2018 (Las Vegas, 442, Nov. 13, 2017), 2019 (Las Vegas, 276, second-most; the release has no date on the dateline), 2020 (New York, 442, Sept. 17, 2019), 2021 (New York, 442, Dec. 4, 2020), 2022 (Washington, 178, third-most, Dec. 19, 2021), 2023 (Indiana, 442, Nov. 11, 2022), 2024 (Indiana, 442, Dec. 10, 2023), 2025 (Dallas, 227, Nov. 17, 2024), and 2026 (Dallas, 420, Nov. 23, 2025).

    On the 2025 results page, the prose says Washington was 33-47 over 2023 and 2024. The table on the same page says 33-43. Both give Washington 104 chances. Dallas's 227 and Los Angeles's 442, the numbers in the count, appear in the prose and in the table.

    No results page opened for this file names the 2011 or 2012 lottery winner. Those two rows stay blank.

    When it is, and where to watch

    The Oct. 28, 2026 release sets the 26th annual lottery for Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, from 7:30 to 8:00 p.m. ET, on ESPN, after UConn vs. Louisville. WNBA head of league operations Bethany Donaphin reveals the results. Ernst & Young oversees the drawing. TSN carries it in Canada. The same release says this is the 15th straight year an ESPN network has carried the lottery. The draft is Monday, April 12, 2027. The sponsor line on the release is State Street Investment Management SPY.

    TV: ESPN, 7:30-8:00 p.m. ET, after UConn vs. Louisville. Streaming: the Oct. 28 release does not name a U.S. app. In Canada, TSN.

    On Nov. 8, 2026, the @WNBA account posted "ONE WEEK OUT" and the same date and time, 7:30 p.m. ET on ESPN. The post's timestamp is 16:00 UTC.

    Six clubs take part: Houston, Los Angeles, Portland, Seattle, Toronto, and Washington. Phoenix traded its first-round pick to Los Angeles. Chicago traded its first-round selection to Washington. The seven non-playoff records are Chicago, Houston (previously the Connecticut Sun), Los Angeles, Phoenix, and Seattle on the combined 2025 and 2026 records, and Portland and Toronto on 2026 only. Portland and Toronto are the 2026 expansion teams. Their rosters and the move are on the expansion page.

    The May 13, 2026 sale release says the WNBA and NBA boards approved the sale of the Connecticut Sun from the Mohegan Tribe to Tilman J. Fertitta, and that the team relocates to Houston for the 2027 season. That release does not put a team name in the text. The WNBA.com menu, opened with the lottery release on Nov. 8, 2026, lists a 2027 Houston Comets team next to the current Sun. The lottery table itself says Houston. Coaching for the Sun, and the open jobs in New York and Los Angeles, are in the 2027 head-coach file.

    Where the two traded picks came from

    Washington's row is Chicago's record, 26-62, and Chicago's 161 combinations. The Sky release of Feb. 23, 2025, says Chicago acquired Ariel Atkins and Washington acquired the 2025 No. 3 pick, Chicago's 2027 second-round pick, and the right to swap 2027 first-round picks. The Mystics release the same day says the swap is Washington's own 2027 first-round pick with Chicago's own. The Oct. 28 lottery release says Chicago traded its own 2027 first-round pick to Washington for Atkins, and it assigns the 161 combinations to Washington.

    Washington is not in the lottery on its own record. The release lists Washington only through Chicago. Chicago's pick is one of the seven lottery slots. A swap of Washington's own pick for Chicago's own pick puts that slot with Washington, which is the assignment the lottery release prints.

    Los Angeles's second ticket is Phoenix's record, 43-45, and 12 combinations. The lottery release says Phoenix traded its own 2027 first-round pick and Monique Akoa Makani to Los Angeles for Kelsey Plum on Aug. 2, 2026. Service time for players whose deals are up is in the 2027 free-agent file.

    What the release does not say about the draft class

    The lottery release does not name a player. It assigns picks, not prospects.

    USC's 2026-27 roster lists JuJu Watkins as a senior, a guard, and says she redshirted the 2025-26 season because of injury. The personal line on that page says she was born July 15, 2005, so she turns 22 in 2027.

    Article XIII, Section 1(b) of the WNBPA collective bargaining agreement says a player is eligible for the draft if she will be at least 22 during the calendar year of the draft, or if she has graduated or her original class is graduating within three months after the draft, and if she has no remaining college eligibility or renounces it in writing at least 10 days before the draft. A shorter window applies after her last NCAA game. This page has the birth date and the senior listing. It does not have a renunciation letter, and it does not have an NCAA eligibility ledger. The redshirt year means this page cannot say she is out of college eligibility. It does not say she will be in the April 12, 2027 draft.
