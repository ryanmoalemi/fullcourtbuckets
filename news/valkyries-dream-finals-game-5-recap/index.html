    Valkyries beat Dream 64-55 in Game 5 and move one win from the WNBA title

    By Ryan MoalemiOctober 26, 2026

    The Golden State Valkyries beat the Atlanta Dream 64-55 in Game 5 of the WNBA Finals on Monday at Chase Center, taking a 3-2 series lead. Golden State is one win from the title.

    Atlanta made 19 of 65 shots, 29.2%, and 6 of 26 threes. Its 55 points are the fewest by either team in the series. Golden State shot 39.3% and blocked 9 shots.

    Atlanta's 8-point quarter

    The Dream led 14-13 after one quarter on a Rhyne Howard three with 21.5 seconds left. That one-point edge was Atlanta's biggest lead of the night. The Dream scored 8 points in the second quarter and trailed 25-22 at halftime. Their 22 first-half points are the fewest by either team in a half this series.

    Kiah Stokes made two threes in the third quarter, and Golden State won it 21-18 to lead 46-40.

    How Golden State pulled away

    A Jordin Canada layup cut the lead to 46-42 with 9:53 left. Golden State then scored 10 of the next 12 points: a Gabby Williams and-one, a Stokes three at 7:21 assisted by Cecilia Zandalasini, a Veronica Burton basket and a Zandalasini basket at 4:58 for 56-44.

    After two Isobel Borlase free throws, Tiffany Hayes scored Golden State's next 6 points, a basket at 3:50 assisted by Zandalasini and four free throws. Her last two, at 1:46, made it 62-46, the Valkyries' biggest lead. Atlanta did not score from 4:07 to 1:30, then hit three late threes, two by Canada and one by Howard, to close the gap.

    The numbers that decided it

    • Turnovers. Atlanta 15, Golden State 7 (official team totals). Golden State had 9 steals.
    • Shooting. Golden State made 24 of 61 shots and Atlanta 19 of 65. Both teams made 6 threes.
    • Rim protection. Golden State blocked 9 shots to Atlanta's 3. Burton had 4, Stokes 3, and Kaila Charles and Zandalasini 1 each.
    • The glass. Atlanta won the rebounding battle 44-33, with 12 offensive rebounds to Golden State's 5, but did not turn the extra chances into points.

    Stokes had 13 points on 5 of 6 shooting, 3 of 4 from three, with 8 rebounds and 3 blocks. Burton added 13 points, 8 rebounds, 3 assists and 4 blocks. Hayes scored 12 on 4 of 9 shooting and 4 of 4 free throws. Zandalasini had 12 points off the bench on 5 of 10 shooting and was +9, the best mark of the game. Williams scored 9 with 3 steals.

    Howard led Atlanta with 20 points on 7 of 15 shooting, including 3 of 8 threes. Angel Reese had 15 rebounds, 5 of them offensive, and 5 assists, but scored 7 points on 2 of 8 shooting. Canada scored 12 on 5 of 16, Allisha Gray had 10 on 4 of 12, and Naz Hillmon grabbed 11 rebounds.

    Watch every possession on the Game 5 play-by-play page, or check the official WNBA.com box score.

    The series so far

    The team with fewer turnovers has won all five games, and so has the team leading at halftime. Atlanta has won its two games by a combined 40 points. Golden State has won its three by a combined 18.

    Game 5 drew 18,064 at Chase Center, a sellout. That brings the 2026 Finals to 88,390 fans through five games, according to the FCB Finals attendance tracker. The record is 92,599, set in 2024. A Game 6 crowd of 4,209 would tie it and 4,210 would break it. State Farm Arena drew 17,099 for Game 4.

    What's next

    Game 6 is Thursday, Oct. 29, at 9:30 a.m. PT (12:30 p.m. ET) at State Farm Arena in Atlanta (WNBA.com game page), on USA Network, E! and Syfy and streaming on Peacock. Golden State can clinch the title with a win.
