Top 10 Women's College Basketball Players to Collect in 2026

By Ryan Moalemi

JuJu Watkins leads the list, and a collection can still start with someone else on it. These are the players, specific cards and collecting stories worth knowing before the next wave of WNBA rookies arrives.

1. JuJu Watkins, USC

Watkins is my first choice for the player whose college story could develop into a much broader collecting audience. That is a ceiling call. Her cards can still be expensive. The basketball expectations remain substantial: the Big Ten named her its 2026–27 preseason player of the year after she missed the previous season through injury.

The draw is following a college headliner into a major professional career, and the price can already include that career. Her return makes this a season to watch closely. Each parallel has to earn its own price.

The card to study: 2024–25 Bowman University Chrome Purple Lava #37

This Purple Lava example is useful because it forces a collector to be specific. It is a college parallel, not a WNBA rookie card, and the pictured card does not carry a 1st Bowman badge. Purple Lava, ordinary base Chrome and other refractor versions should not be treated as interchangeable purchases.

SportsCardsPro's page for this Purple Lava #37 records a PSA 10 sale at $42.01 on July 15, 2026. The listing title is "2024 BOWMAN CHROME UNIVERSITY PURPLE LAVA #37 JUJU WATKINS PSA 10." That is a dated reference. Compare newer sales of the same parallel and grade before using it to make an offer.

What would strengthen the case: a successful return followed by sustained top-level play. What could undermine it: paying a superstar premium for a card whose particular version never becomes a collector favorite.

2. Sarah Strong, UConn

Strong offers a collecting story built on accomplishments she already has. Her UConn résumé includes the 2025 national championship and the 2026 Naismith, Wooden and AP player-of-the-year awards. She enters her junior season with a case that already makes sense to someone who follows the sport but has never bought a basketball card.

That is why she ranks second here. A collection can connect her early UConn years to titles, awards and whatever comes next. I would rather start with that identifiable history than pay extra for an obscure parallel solely because its serial number is small.

The card to study: 2024–25 Bowman University Chrome #62

The UConn uniform and visible 1st Bowman badge give #62 a straightforward role in an early-career collection. The image below is the non-autographed base card. A seller's autograph, numbered refractor or Sapphire listing needs its own comparison set.

Before paying a large grading premium, compare raw copies with eBay sold results for Strong #62 in PSA 10. Check the photographs and parallel names, not just the search results' prices.

The limit: correctly identifying a great player does not tell you which of her many cards will retain the strongest demand. Start with an issue you can explain and a price you can defend.

3. Hannah Hidalgo, Notre Dame

Hidalgo has a particularly clear identity: she scores, disrupts possessions and produces unusual stat lines. According to Notre Dame's official player biography, she averaged 25.3 points and 5.6 steals in 2025–26 and won both ACC player-of-the-year and defensive-player-of-the-year honors.

For collectors, that is more memorable than a vague claim that someone has potential. Her case depends on continuing to make that identity matter on bigger stages.

The card to study: 2024–25 Bowman University Chrome #26

This card ties Hidalgo directly to Notre Dame, with an in-game photograph and the school identity built into the design. It is a sensible reference point for a college-focused collection. Notice that the pictured #26 does not show a 1st Bowman badge. Do not add one because a listing calls it an early card.

The line under the photo is that sale. Then verify the version in each listing. A numbered refractor appearing in a broad search is not evidence for the value of this base card.

Price check: college excellence and professional collecting demand are different tests. Hidalgo belongs near the top of this watchlist, and her college awards still leave the resale price to the sales you can find.

4. Madison Booker, Texas

Booker's case is built on repeatable production and a substantial awards record. Texas lists her as a three-time Cheryl Miller small-forward-of-the-year winner. Her 2025–26 season also included AP first-team All-America honors and SEC tournament MVP.

She is my pick for collectors who would rather follow an accomplished scoring wing than buy primarily around a viral moment. Her basketball case can stand on its own. The card price is a separate question.

The card to study: 2025–26 Bowman Chrome Prospects #BCP-15

The pictured card has a 1st Bowman badge and shows Booker in Texas orange. More importantly, its identifier is BCP-15. The official Topps checklist separates that Chrome card from paper prospect BPP-15 and Chrome Prospect Autograph CPA-MB.

This is exactly where knowing the checklist can save money. A search for "Madison Booker 1st Bowman" can mix several different products. Start with Topps' Bowman Basketball product information, then compare the actual card number and parallel.

Before you buy: the right player can still be the wrong version at the wrong comparison price. A good professional career would not make those three card types interchangeable.

5. Mikayla Blakes, Vanderbilt

Blakes has already supplied the sort of performances collectors hope to get from a breakout candidate. Vanderbilt records that she led the nation with 27.0 points per game in 2025–26 and won SEC player of the year. Her freshman season included 55 points at Auburn on February 16, 2025.

That makes her especially interesting for collectors who like cards connected to a specific achievement. The card below already has its milestone. You can explain it from a night that happened.

The card to study: 2024–25 Bowman U NOW #70

This issue commemorates her freshman single-game scoring record. Topps' official #70 archive page reports a print run of 2,168. That is a manufacturer-published figure for this issue, not a count of graded copies or individual owners.

The product's "look for relics and auto-relics" language describes possible special cards, not what every base-card buyer receives. Buy the pictured base card. The exciting phrase in the product description can belong to a different card.

The opportunity: a memorable early milestone attached to a proven scorer. The hang-up: a great game does not guarantee that this particular NOW issue becomes her most sought-after card.

6. Joyce Edwards, South Carolina

Edwards has already moved well beyond prospect-only credentials. She averaged 19.2 points and 6.8 rebounds in 2025–26, earned AP second-team All-America honors and set South Carolina's single-season scoring record with 768 points.

Her collecting appeal has two potential audiences: people following Edwards herself and people documenting South Carolina basketball. A card tied to a team achievement can make sense to both without needing a future WNBA projection to justify its place in a collection.

The card to study: 2024–25 Bowman U NOW #80

The net-cutting photograph is the point of this card. It commemorates South Carolina's March 9, 2025 SEC tournament title, rather than simply putting Edwards on another generic design. Topps identifies the issue as #80 and reports a print run of 412.

There is a useful comparison here: 412 is substantially below the published 2,168 for Blakes #70. A print run counts how many were made. The sale price shows who wanted one. SportsCardsPro's #80 page had no completed sale when this was checked, so there is no sold price under this photo. The guide price on that page is not a sale.

Where it can slip: a small print run can be mistaken for a large market. This is a particularly appealing team-history collectible. Scarcity still needs buyers.

7. MiLaysia Fulwiley, LSU

Fulwiley is the style-of-play pick. Her creative handling and attacking game give a casual viewer an immediate reason to keep watching. There is production behind it: LSU records 14.6 points per game and a second consecutive SEC Sixth Woman of the Year award in 2025–26.

My collecting case is that memorable play can build an audience beyond people studying draft boards. Attention is only the opening. To support lasting card demand, that audience has to remain interested through changing roles and the transition to professional basketball.

The card to study: 2023 Topps Chrome McDonald's All American #77

This pre-college issue shows Fulwiley in her McDonald's East uniform, holding a basketball above an inset portrait. It belongs to a different collecting category than a South Carolina or LSU college card. That can be useful when building a career timeline: the card's identity does not depend on which college she currently attends.

Later rows on the SportsCardsPro page include /75 copies, which are a different buy from the base card in the photo. For available copies, use a search specific to Fulwiley's 2023 Chrome #77. Listing prices are sellers' requests, not completed-sale evidence. Confirm whether the listing is base, a numbered parallel or a multi-card lot.

The catch: a spectacular highlight can create a temporary price spike. Watch the player. Be picky about which card you buy.

8. Mikaylah Williams, LSU

Williams gives collectors another LSU candidate with a different case. Her official biography lists first-team All-SEC honors in both 2025 and 2026. In 2025–26, she averaged 14.2 points, 5.3 rebounds and 3.6 assists.

The question is whether sustained production turns into greater individual recognition. I would watch that development on its own. The appealing part is that an early-card collection does not have to begin with a premium autograph.

The card to study: 2023 Topps Chrome McDonald's All American #47

The action photograph and inset portrait give this card a clear pre-college identity. It shows Williams in the McDonald's West uniform, not LSU colors. For a collector interested in the beginning of her career, that distinction is part of the appeal.

That page records a reported eBay sale of $1.99 on August 1, 2026, titled "2023 Topps Chrome McDonald's All American #47 Mikaylah Williams #47." It is one historical transaction for this base card. Collecting an early card and making a large speculative bet are different activities.

One check: a player can improve while her individual card market stays quiet. Match what you pay to the sales you can actually point to.

9. Audi Crooks, Oklahoma State

First, the roster update: Crooks is now at Oklahoma State, following three seasons at Iowa State. Her new school's biography credits her with 25.8 points per game and 64.9 percent shooting in 2025–26.

Those numbers explain the basketball appeal. The collecting question is how her inside scoring translates to the next level, and whether her individual audience grows through the move. I rank her below the guards and wings above because I would want more evidence on that professional transition before making a major speculative purchase.

The card to study: 2024–25 Bowman University Chrome #9

This 1st Bowman shows Crooks in an Iowa State uniform. It is not an Oklahoma State card with an incorrect photograph. It documents the earlier chapter of her career, which is exactly why a collector might choose it.

A player-focused collection can include both college chapters. A school-focused collector may prefer one uniform. The older card still documents the Iowa State chapter. A transfer does not, by itself, raise the price.

The caution: college scoring can be projected straight into professional stardom. Check sales of this actual card before paying extra for the transfer story.

10. Jaloni Cambridge, Ohio State

Cambridge closes the list as a player to follow before making stronger claims about her hobby market. The Big Ten's 2026–27 preseason announcement places the Ohio State junior on both all-conference teams, with unanimous recognition from the media panel.

That gives the basketball case a concrete foundation. My collecting interest is in whether she can turn that conference standing into a larger national profile. This is a name to follow while the listings teach you the card.

The card to study: 2024–25 Bowman University Chrome #29

The visible 1st Bowman badge and Ohio State uniform make this a straightforward starting point. The base card below also provides a design reference before comparing more complicated refractors or graded copies.

Start by following the same version over several sales. A lone high result, especially for a different parallel, is not enough to establish a trend. The useful development would be repeat buyer interest alongside her continued basketball progress.

Keep in mind: future recognition is something to watch through the season.

The bottom line

Watkins is my top crossover-upside pick. Strong has the most compelling championship-and-awards combination behind her early UConn cards. Hidalgo and Booker offer substantial basketball résumés, while Blakes and Edwards have specific milestone issues that are interesting even without a resale prediction.

None of that requires buying all ten. Follow the players you care about, learn each card well enough to catch a bad comparison, and let the collection grow with the games.
