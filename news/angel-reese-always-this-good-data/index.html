    Angel Reese had 21 points, 12 rebounds and 3 steals on 7-of-12 shooting as the Dream beat the Valkyries 97-71 in Game 3 on Wednesday for a 2-1 Finals lead (WNBA box score, FCB play-by-play). During the game, at 5:44 PM PT, Colin Salao posted the line the replies ran with:

    “It’s actually insane how quickly Angel Reese got this good”

    Colin Salao (@colincsalao on X), Oct. 21, 2026, 5:44 PM PT

    By 9:41 PM PT that night the post had 22,505 views, 212 likes, 84 replies and 29 quote posts. So we went back and logged all 116 of her college games from the Maryland and LSU box scores, plus her eight 2026 playoff games. She had 10 or more rebounds in 80 of the 116 college games. The rebounding was never the thing that changed. Her shooting was.

    What the ledger shows

    1. The 2022-23 run is the outlier, and it was in college. Reese had a double-double in 34 of 36 LSU games that season, 23 of them in a row from the opener, and six games with 20 or more rebounds, topped by 26 points and 28 rebounds against Texas A&M on Jan. 5, 2023 (LSU stats PDF). LSU lists the 34 double-doubles as an NCAA single-season record (LSU bio). Across four college seasons she had seven 20-point, 20-rebound games.
    2. Maryland already had the rebounder. As a sophomore she had 17 double-doubles in 32 games and a seven-game run of 10-plus rebounds. Half of her rebounds that season (169 of 340) were offensive, the highest share of any season in the table (Maryland stats). Her freshman year was 15 games after a fractured right foot (Maryland notes).
    3. No WNBA player who debuted since 2009 has rebounded like this over a first three seasons. In FCB's season data, Reese has 1,344 rebounds in 107 games, 12.6 a game. Next is Tina Charles at 11.1 (2010-12), then Teaira McCowan and Breanna Stewart at 8.8 and Aliyah Boston at 8.5 (minimum 60 games over the first three seasons, 215 players). Reese also had the top rebounding average among players with 20 or more games in each of 2024, 2025 and 2026.
    4. 2026 was the record year. She set WNBA season records with 521 rebounds, 206 offensive rebounds and 32 double-doubles, became the first player to 500 rebounds in a season, and grabbed a league-record 26 against the Sparks on Aug. 24 (WNBA, AP). She was named Defensive Player of the Year with 46 of 74 votes (WNBA).
    5. The jump is shooting. Her true shooting fell to 46.4% as a rookie, then went to 53.7% and 52.4%, and is 59.7% in eight 2026 playoff games (155 points on 106 shots and 54 free throws), the highest of the periods in the table. Eight games is a small sample, and the three Finals games are part of it. In the Finals she is 22 of 39 (56.4%) with 65 points and 40 rebounds (WNBA box scores: Game 1, Game 2, Game 3).

    Does she finish seasons stronger?

    One reply said her finishing took a leap later in the season. We can't split the 2026 WNBA regular season by month yet, but the college box scores let us check whether she usually gets better late. She didn't.

    In all three full college seasons her shooting fell in the second half. In 2026 it went the other way: 45.1% in the regular season, 48.1% in the playoffs and 56.4% in the Finals. That is a change from her own pattern. It is still field-goal percentage, which mixes every shot type, so it can't prove her finishing improved. Showing that needs shot-location data and film.

    What the replies got right and wrong

    We read the replies and quote posts in the thread. Each post below was checked on X on Oct. 21 at 9:41 PM PT, and each claim is tested against the data.

    “Overnight”

    “😂 it’s like she came out of no where.”

    @tisagraves on X

    “Been doing this since high school yall just love to sound stupid”

    @sdpmook on X

    The data backs @sdpmook. A No. 2 national recruit with 79 college double-doubles and three seasons atop the WNBA rebounding table did not come out of nowhere. We found no primary source for her junior high stats, so that era is left out.

    “Only rebounds and defense”

    “The defense and rebounding were always there. Coming into her own as a scorer and the go-to for her team.”

    @scism_cameron on X

    Mostly right, with one fix: she was a scorer before. She averaged 23.0 points at LSU in 2022-23 and led the SEC (LSU stats, LSU bio). What dipped was her rookie WNBA scoring, 13.6 points on 39.1% shooting. It has gone up every season since: 14.7, 16.4, then 19.4 in the playoffs.

    “She isn't a shooter”

    “She is surrounded by a good team. She isn't a shooter. Just had some good looks tonight.”

    @zechariah1680 on X

    Half right. She hit 2 of 2 threes in Game 3, but shot 19.3% on 1.3 threes a game in the 2026 regular season (WNBA). She is 8 of 17 from three in the playoffs, too few to call. In 116 college games she took 32 threes. Her points come at the rim and the line, and she has made 45 of 54 free throws (83.3%) in the playoffs after 72.1% in the regular season.

    “It's foul calls”

    “Yeah? It’s called foul calls”

    @justtradebaby on X

    Box scores can't settle this. Free-throw counts show how often she got to the line, not whether any call was right. Here is what they do show. She is taking 6.8 free throws a game in the playoffs and took 6.3 in the regular season. At LSU she took 9.4 a game in 2022-23 and 8.1 in 2023-24 (2023, 2024). Per shot, her free-throw rate has dropped too: 0.61 and 0.60 free throws per field-goal try at LSU, 0.49 in the 2026 regular season, 0.51 in the playoffs. She is getting to the line less often per shot than at LSU and making more of the free throws she takes.

    “She went to a loaded team”

    “She went to an already loaded team cut it out 😂😂 check her record with the team that drafted her 😂😂”

    @r8der_g on X

    She did change teams in a trade from the Sky to the Dream (Chicago Sky), and Atlanta was already a 30-win team in 2025 (WNBA). Her own numbers went up in Atlanta: 12.9 shots a game vs. 11.1 in 2025, 16.4 points vs. 14.7, and 3.1 turnovers vs. 3.9. A better roster and her own growth can both be true, and these numbers can't separate the two. We did not verify the Sky's team records for this piece, so we don't compare them.

    “3x All-Star”

    “She’s a 3x All-Star, she’s been good stupid”

    @wnbawanksta on X

    Correct. The WNBA calls 2026 her third straight All-Star selection (WNBA), and LSU lists 2024, 2025 and 2026 (LSU).

    “Her finishing took a leap”

    “Angel Reese has always been an impact player. But her finishing took a leap later in the season and that’s been the difference.”

    @robinlundberg on X

    The data fits this, but can't prove it. See the split above: her shooting usually faded late in college seasons, and in 2026 it rose from 45.1% to 48.1% in the playoffs and 56.4% in the Finals. A month-by-month 2026 regular-season split is still missing, so we can't say which month it started, and FG% alone doesn't isolate finishing.

    Why it looks overnight

    • Injuries cut her samples short. A fractured right foot limited her to 15 games as a Maryland freshman (Maryland). A wrist fracture that needed surgery ended her rookie WNBA season after 34 games (AP). She played 30 games in 2025 (WNBA).
    • Her weak stretch was her rookie shooting. The 39.1% rookie season is the low point in the table, and it came in her first WNBA year. Her rebounding that year set a league record.
    • The spotlight was on more than basketball. LSU says her Instagram grew from about 70,000 followers when she arrived to nearly 500,000 entering the 2023 NCAA tournament and more than 1 million after it (LSU), and Reebok announced a signature shoe for her (Authentic Brands). We did not measure how much coverage was about off-court topics, so this is context, not proof.
    • The big audience isn't new. Her 2023 title game drew 9.9 million viewers on ABC (ESPN press release). Finals Game 1 this year drew 2.1 million (NBC Sports) and Game 2 drew 1.33 million (USA Sports). We had not found a Game 3 audience figure as of Oct. 21.
