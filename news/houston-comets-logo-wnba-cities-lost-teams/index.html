    Houston Comets unveil new logo as the second of 4 cities to win back a lost WNBA team

    By Ryan MoalemiOctober 30, 2026

    The Houston Comets unveiled their new logo on Oct. 29. Houston is the second of four cities in a row to win back a WNBA team it lost.

    Every WNBA city that lost a team, and the four getting one back

    Since the league began play in 1997, twelve markets have lost a WNBA team. Portland got a team back in 2026, Houston follows in 2027, Cleveland in 2028 and Detroit in 2029. Houston's 18-season wait is the shortest of the four.

    What the new Comets logo shows

    The team revealed the logo at an event at POST Houston on Thursday night. "It's our turn to make history. The Comets are back," the team posted on X. Its first post of the reveal, "Allow us to reintroduce ourselves," drew about 9,800 likes by Friday morning (Houston Comets on X).

    The primary mark is a navy "H" slanting upward, with a red basketball streaking through it like a comet. Four large stars sit inside the H for the original team's four titles. Three are silver, and the white one stands for Texas, the Lone Star State (SportsLogos.net, Rockets Wire).

    A second, round logo puts the H on a red badge inspired by space mission patches, SportsLogos.net reports. The "HOUSTON" and "COMETS" wordmarks use a lowercase "t," which the team ties to the shared history of the Rockets and Comets. The colors are red, silver and navy. Sports Business Journal reported that Torch Creative of Dallas designed the identity (SBJ). Uniforms have not been released, and the team says more brand elements will come in the next few months (Rockets Wire).

    Comets Hall of Famers Van Chancellor, Cynthia Cooper, Sheryl Swoopes and Tina Thompson attended the reveal. Chancellor, Cooper and Swoopes are scheduled to appear at a pop-up shop at the George R. Brown Convention Center this weekend (Rockets Wire).

    Why the Comets name is on a different franchise

    The original Comets won the first four WNBA titles, 1997 through 2000 (WNBA). Four titles is still tied for the most by any franchise, with the Minnesota Lynx and Seattle Storm. That team suspended operations on Dec. 2, 2008, and its players went out in a dispersal draft a week later (WNBA history timeline).

    The team heading to Houston is a different franchise. It began as the Orlando Miracle in 1999 and moved to Connecticut as the Sun in 2003 (WNBA history timeline). Mohegan's 2003 membership agreement with the WNBA gave the new team "all of the rights to current and future players" the Miracle would have had and named it the "Connecticut Sun" (SEC filing).

    On May 13, 2026, the WNBA and NBA Board of Governors unanimously approved the sale of the Connecticut Sun to Tilman J. Fertitta and the team's move to Houston for 2027 (WNBA). Team officials said the original Comets' history and records will carry forward (KPRC 2). The team will play at Toyota Center.

    That makes the Orlando franchise the third in WNBA history to move twice. The Utah Starzz became the San Antonio Stars and then the Las Vegas Aces, and the Detroit Shock went to Tulsa and then became the Dallas Wings (WNBA, WNBA).

    From a $10 million fee to a $300 million sale

    Mohegan agreed to pay the WNBA a $10,000,000 expansion fee for the Connecticut membership in 2003, according to the agreement it filed with the SEC (SEC filing). When the Fertitta sale closed on May 14, 2026, Mohegan said it would receive $300 million in total, with $150 million at closing and the rest due by Dec. 31, 2026 (Mohegan).

    That is 30 times the 2003 figure, before inflation. The two numbers come from different kinds of deals: one was a fee paid to the league to join, and the other was the sale of a running team.

    Four cities getting their teams back

    Of the 29 WNBA titles from 1997 to 2025, eight were won in cities that later lost their teams: four in Houston, three in Detroit and one in Sacramento (WNBA). With Houston and Detroit coming back, seven of those eight titles were won in cities that will have a team again.

    The Portland Fire played three seasons, from 2000 to 2002, and Portland returned in 2026 after more than 20 years (WNBA). The league also added the Toronto Tempo in 2026 and the Golden State Valkyries in 2025 (WNBA, WNBA). The Board of Governors approved Cleveland for 2028, Detroit for 2029 and Philadelphia for 2030 (WNBA). Cleveland's team is the Sirens (Rock Entertainment Group).

    Connecticut is next on the list. After 24 seasons at Mohegan Sun Arena, it has no announced return. For every expansion team and its first season, see our WNBA expansion teams tracker. Houston's first pick as the Comets comes in the 2027 WNBA draft lottery.
