    Kelsey Mitchell 2027: cap paths for three WNBA teams

    By Ryan MoalemiNovember 8, 2026

    Golden State has the cleanest route to signing Kelsey Mitchell without trading a contracted player, but Las Vegas can also fit her if Jackie Young leaves. Atlanta cannot keep its nine listed contracts and add Mitchell at the standard maximum under either cap assumption tested here.

    The important wrinkle is A'ja Wilson's contract. Her 2027 salary floats at 20% of the cap, according to Her Hoop Stats. That makes the Aces' three-star build more expensive than simply carrying forward the database's displayed $1.47 million figure. Even trading Jewell Loyd for no incoming salary would leave Las Vegas short in a Wilson-Young-Mitchell maximum-salary build.

    These are hypothetical roster budgets, not reported offers or a list of Mitchell's preferred destinations. Indiana remains the financial benchmark: at a $7.91 million cap, its maximum starting salary for Mitchell would exceed a new team's by $237,300 under the signed WNBA-WNBPA agreement.

    Why Indiana can offer more

    Mitchell has completed nine WNBA seasons, all in Indiana, and will enter the market as an unrestricted free agent, as IndyStar reported. Beginning in 2027, the CBA limits new core designations to players with six or fewer years of service. Indiana cannot use a new core designation to keep her off the market.

    The salary advantage is separate. Article V, Section 8 allows a qualifying veteran free agent with at least five years of service to re-sign with her prior team at up to 20% of the cap. Mitchell's starting maximum with a new team is 17%. Article V, Section 3(f) prevents Indiana from using an agreed sign-and-trade to give her a starting salary above the standard maximum.

    The $7.91 million figure is a ceiling, not an announced cap. The agreement sets the 2026 cap at $7 million and limits the 2027 change in either direction to 13%. The full permitted range is therefore $6.09 million to $7.91 million. The $7.50 million case is an FCB sensitivity test, not a league projection or the lowest possible outcome.

    What Mitchell brings, and what the numbers cannot tell us

    Mitchell scored a WNBA-record 1,086 regular-season points in 2026. Basketball-Reference lists 44 games, 24.7 points and 2.8 assists per game, with shooting percentages of 50.9% from the field, 45.0% from three and 85.1% at the line.

    FCB calculations from Basketball-Reference's season totals: 381 field goals in 748 attempts, including 135 threes in 300 attempts, and 189 free throws in 222 attempts. Effective field-goal percentage is (381 + 0.5 × 135) ÷ 748 = 59.96%, or 60.0% rounded to one decimal. It gives made threes their extra scoring value but does not include free throws.

    The passing context matters. An Indiana Fever release republished by OurSports Central credits Clark with 96 assists to Mitchell out of 333 total assists, or 28.8%. That is the share of Clark's assists that went to Mitchell, not the share of Mitchell's baskets assisted by Clark. It shows a productive partnership, not how much of Mitchell's production would disappear elsewhere.

    There is also a small postseason caution. Mitchell scored 61 points in three games against Las Vegas, averaging 20.3, while shooting 18-for-55, or 32.7%, according to Basketball-Reference. Indiana lost the series 2-1. Three games do not establish her playoff ceiling or prove what caused the missed shots. They do explain why a new team should keep a second creator rather than ask Mitchell to solve every late-clock possession.

    Golden State: add scoring without dismantling the contracted roster

    The Valkyries went 32-12 and beat Atlanta 68-63 in Game 7 of the Finals. Basketball-Reference's possession estimates put them first in defensive rating, at 100.6 points allowed per 100 possessions, and seventh in offensive rating, at 110.0. Those estimates should not be mixed with the league's separately calculated ratings.

    Their 2027 contract list explains the appeal:

    Williams made the 2026 All-Defensive First Team; Burton and Stokes made the Second Team. That offers a reasonable starting point for protecting a scoring guard. It does not remove opponents' ability to target her in ball screens.

    One possible closing group: Burton, Mitchell, Williams, Salaün and Stokes. Burton could organize the offense while Mitchell comes off screens. Against a blitz, the release pass would need to find Williams or another teammate quickly enough to attack the temporary four-on-three advantage. On defense, Burton and Williams could take difficult perimeter assignments, but the group would still need clear help rules behind Mitchell.

    A smaller alternative: replace Stokes with Charles and move Salaün to center. That could spread the floor, but calling it automatic five-out spacing would overstate the evidence. Opponents must respect each player's shot for that structure to work, and a smaller group still has to rebound.

    Golden State's remaining decision is who returns. Her Hoop Stats lists Tiffany Hayes and Kayla Thornton as unrestricted free agents, and Cecilia Zandalasini as restricted. The model does not retain them at their 2026 salaries. Any amount spent above the minimum to bring them back reduces the $800,972 surplus at the upper cap assumption, or $460,672 at $7.50 million.

    FCB's judgment: this is the strongest combination of retained defensive personnel and available budget among these three teams. Keeping the seven contracted players is different from keeping the championship rotation intact.

    Las Vegas: Wilson's floating salary changes the answer

    The Aces went 31-13 before Golden State swept them 3-0 in the semifinals. Wilson averaged 26.2 points and 9.4 rebounds; Young averaged 18.9 points, 6.8 assists and 4.4 rebounds, according to Basketball-Reference.

    Young is listed as a 2027 unrestricted free agent. The five existing contracts are Wilson, Chelsea Gray, Loyd, Dana Evans and Janiah Barker. The database displays $4,047,600 in total, including $1,470,000 for Wilson, but its Wilson contract note says her future salary will be 20% of that year's cap.

    If Young leaves: Mitchell and Wilson give Becky Hammon an appealing pick-and-roll option, with Gray available to initiate or make the next pass. A defense that steps out to Mitchell risks opening a passing lane to Wilson. That is an opportunity, not a prediction that the pairing would be the league's best.

    Las Vegas could sign Mitchell at 17% and budget six veteran minimum slots, leaving $674,100 at a $7.91 million cap or $415,800 at $7.50 million. The basketball cost is Young's departure. Replacing her scoring does not automatically replace her playmaking, size or defensive role, and cap room alone does not guarantee starting-caliber replacements.

    If Young stays at 20%: the budget is short by $619,300 at $7.91 million or $795,600 at $7.50 million after five minimum slots. This is a stress test of a maximum-salary build, not an assumption that Young will demand or receive that salary.

    Loyd's $840,000 salary illustrates the difficulty. Trading it for no returning salary saves $551,400 after a $288,600 replacement charge. That still leaves a $67,900 shortfall at $7.91 million, or $244,200 at $7.50 million. A Loyd-only move does not fund this version of the roster.

    FCB's judgment: replacing Young with Mitchell fits both budgets. Keeping both at their modeled maximums requires more than moving Loyd alone, and any additional move has a basketball cost.

    Keep all nine contracts: at the $7.91 million cap, Atlanta could allocate $1,151,970 to Mitchell after two veteran minimum slots.     FCB's judgment: Atlanta's question is whether the scoring upgrade is worth the player or salary concession needed to create it. The cap model identifies the cost. It cannot decide whether a trade partner exists or what Mitchell values most.

    Indiana is the comparison every outside offer has to beat

    Clark has publicly said Indiana will recruit Mitchell to return. The Fever offer an established partnership with Clark, continuity with Aliyah Boston and the possibility of a higher starting salary. None of that guarantees a contract agreement.

    The WNBA seeds its top eight playoff teams regardless of conference, so each contender needs answers to more than one opponent. For Golden State, the test is whether extra scoring survives without eroding the defense. For Las Vegas, it is who remains around Wilson after the salary decisions. For Atlanta, it is whether the team can add Mitchell without losing the passing or frontcourt minutes that the new lineup needs.

    The cap budgets make Golden State the most straightforward outside option in this comparison. They do not make it Mitchell's choice.

    Methodology and limitations

    Full Court Buckets transcribed the 2027 contract columns from Her Hoop Stats and independently summed the player-level inputs. Data through November 7, 2026 (PST). The three cap sheets showed an update on October 1, 2026, at 9:35 a.m. PDT. The calculations are a snapshot of those public records, not a guarantee that the rosters will remain unchanged.

    For each team and cap assumption:

    Remaining cap room = C - S(C) - (0.17 × C) - Y(C) - ($288,600 × O)

    • C is the assumed 2027 team salary cap: $7,500,000 or $7,910,000.
    • S(C) is the combined 2027 cap hit of the existing contracts retained in the scenario. It is $4,609,928 for Golden State and $6,180,830 for Atlanta.
    • For Las Vegas, S(C) = $4,047,600 - $1,470,000 + (0.20 × C). The subtracted amount is the database's displayed Wilson figure. Her contract note says the actual future salary is 20% of that year's cap, so that floating amount must be substituted before calculating room. The remaining four listed contracts total $2,577,600.
    • Mitchell's modeled first-year salary is 0.17 × C.
    • Y(C) is zero unless the scenario also re-signs Young at 0.20 × C. It is an assumption about a possible contract, not a reported asking price.
    • O is the number of unfilled regular-player positions needed to reach 12 after keeping the existing contracted players, adding Mitchell and, where applicable, adding Young: 12 - existing players - 1 - Young indicator. That produces four minimum slots for Golden State, six for Las Vegas without Young, five for Las Vegas with Young, and two for Atlanta.

    The signed CBA supplies the cap and roster rules. Article V, Section 8 defines the 20% supermax and 17% standard maximum; Section 3(f) limits an agreed sign-and-trade's starting salary. Article VI, Section 7 governs core eligibility. Article VII, Section 1 fixes the $7 million 2026 cap and the 13% maximum movement in 2027. Article XXXIII, Section 4 requires 12 regular players. Article V, Section 7 and Article I's definition of Salary provide the minimum salary and veteran cap treatment.

    Minimum charge is not minimum pay for every veteran. The 2027 minimum salary for players with one to three years of service is $288,600. The minimum salaries for players with four to six, seven to nine, and at least ten years are $296,400, $304,200 and $312,000, respectively, but their minimum contracts receive the one-to-three-year minimum cap charge. Zero-service rookie minimum pay is $280,800. This model fills vacancies with veterans at the $288,600 cap charge; it does not optimize draft selections or rookie-scale contracts.

    Trade arithmetic: New balance = prior balance + outgoing cap hit - incoming cap hit - $288,600. The final term funds the newly opened roster position. Each illustrated move assumes zero incoming salary, a willing trade partner and no incremental bonus or other charge. For Loyd, the savings are $840,000 - $288,600 = $551,400.

    All currently listed contracts and options are treated as retained, including unprotected contracts. Unsigned free agents and unsigned draft rights are excluded, not presumed re-signed for free. Developmental contracts are not used to replace any of the 12 regular-player positions. No new dead money, trade bonus, unannounced extension or other transaction charge is added. Those items, changes in availability or a different mix of rookies and veterans would change the budget. This is a scenario comparison, not a comprehensive league cap-compliance opinion.

    Dollar outputs use unrounded integer inputs. Percentages in the text are rounded to one decimal unless the eFG calculation is shown to two decimals. Cap balances are not forecasts of offers, player preferences, available trade partners or championship odds. Tactical lineup discussions are FCB analysis, not a claim of private scouting or film-based measurement. Team efficiency ratings use Basketball-Reference's possession estimates, which differ from WNBA calculations.

    Editorial disclosure: The supplied draft was AI-generated. AI assisted this source review, arithmetic verification and editing. Full Court Buckets is responsible for the analysis; the linked public records, rather than AI output, are the evidence for factual claims. No private negotiations were used.
