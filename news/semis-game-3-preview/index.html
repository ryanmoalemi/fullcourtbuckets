Semis Game 3 Preview: Can the Liberty and Aces Do What No WNBA Team Has Done?

By Ryan MoalemiOctober 9, 2026

The New York Liberty have won more quarters than the Atlanta Dream in this series, four to two, and they're still down 0-2 (Game 1, Game 2). One column in the box score explains it, and it's the same column that decides whether Brooklyn sees a Game 4.

Both semifinal Game 3s are Friday, and the stakes are simple. Teams that take a 2-0 lead in a best-of-five WNBA series are 19-0 all time. The Liberty and the Las Vegas Aces are both trying to be the first team to come back.

Dream at Liberty: Friday, 4:30 PM PT, Barclays Center, ESPN2

What decided Games 1 and 2. Ball security, both nights. New York has turned it over 38 times in two games, counting team turnovers. Atlanta has 22. The Dream have scored 60 points off those giveaways while the Liberty have scored 24 (G1 box, G2 box). That's a 36-point swing in a series Atlanta leads by a combined 13.

Add 20 offensive boards to New York's 12 and Atlanta has taken 155 shots to New York's 129. So yes, the Liberty are shooting 48.8% to Atlanta's 42.6%. It doesn't matter when the other team gets 26 extra looks. The Dream are also up 29-8 in fast-break points, which is what happens when your misses turn into layups the other way.

The key matchup. Angel Reese against that New York frontcourt. Reese is plus-18 in the series, the best mark on either team, with Jordin Canada at plus-17 and Naz Hillmon at plus-16. On the other side, Marine Fauthoux is the only Liberty player with a positive plus-minus (plus-6). Starters Leonie Fiebich and Pauline Astier are both minus-10.

New York's stars are scoring. Breanna Stewart dropped 35 in Game 2 and Sabrina Ionescu had 27 in Game 1. But Ionescu also had 5 turnovers in Game 1, and Jonquel Jones had 6 in Game 2.

Atlanta's bench has outscored New York's 42-29, and 23 of those came from DeWanna Bonner in Game 1. And they did most of this without their leading scorer. Allisha Gray hurt her left shooting hand in Game 1, played 11 minutes and missed Game 2. As of Thursday evening, she was listed as questionable for Game 3.

What each team has to do. New York needs single-digit turnovers. That's it. Shoot 48% with 10 giveaways and this is a series again. Atlanta needs to keep crashing and keep running, because the half-court numbers favor New York.

The pick: Dream win and head to the Finals. New York has given it away at least 17 times in both games, and that's not a shooting slump that flips on the flight home. It's a habit. Get Gray back even for 20 minutes and Atlanta is done in three.

Valkyries at Aces: Friday, 6:30 PM PT, Michelob ULTRA Arena, Peacock and NBCSN

What decided Games 1 and 2. The glass. The Golden State Valkyries have 27 offensive rebounds to Vegas's 8, and they've outrebounded the Aces 82-50 (G1 box, G2 box). Kiah Stokes hasn't scored a point in this series and still has 21 rebounds.

Then there's the bench. Golden State's reserves have outscored Vegas's 61-21. Tiffany Hayes has 24 of those, including 21 in 17 minutes in Game 2. Janelle Salaun is plus-18 in just 10 minutes, and Kaitlyn Chen is plus-11.

Here's the stat that should scare Golden State. The Valkyries have turned it over 29 times to Vegas's 18, counting team turnovers. They're winning anyway because Vegas hasn't punished it, scoring 24 points off those turnovers to Golden State's 27.

The key matchup. Golden State's starts against Vegas's finishes. The Valkyries are plus-14 in first halves. In second halves, the Aces are plus-1. Golden State won the first quarter of Game 2 by 8, and Vegas answered by winning the fourth by 9 (G2 box).

A'ja Wilson has 48 points in two games and is still minus-11. Chelsea Gray is also minus-11. The Aces' best plus-minus belongs to Kalani Brown at plus-7, while Brianna Turner is minus-16 and Kierstan Bell is minus-15.

Injuries matter here. As of Thursday evening, ESPN listed Jewell Loyd and Stephanie Talbot as day-to-day with left leg injuries (ESPN game page). Loyd didn't play in Game 2. Dana Evans wasn't on ESPN's injury list when we checked, and she played 18 minutes in Game 2.

What each team has to do. Vegas has to box out. You can't give up 17 offensive rebounds like they did in Game 1 and survive. Golden State has to clean up the turnovers and get the same punch from Gabby Williams, who has 45 points in two games, and Kayla Thornton.

The pick: Aces win Game 3. The defending champs won the second half of this series, swept the regular-season meetings 3-0, and get Michelob ULTRA Arena behind them. In Game 2 they outshot Golden State 44% to 41% and had the ball down two at the end. If Wilson and Gray stop digging out of holes, Vegas extends this. If the Aces lose the glass 52-25 like they did in Game 1, nothing else matters.

For the bettors and fantasy players
