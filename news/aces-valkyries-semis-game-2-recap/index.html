Burton From 28, Stokes at the Rim: Valkyries Hold Off the Aces 83-81, Lead Semis 2-0

By Ryan MoalemiOctober 7, 2026

With 41.5 seconds left and the game tied at 76, Veronica Burton pulled up from 28 feet off a Gabby Williams pass and buried it. Then, with 3 seconds left and Vegas down two, Kiah Stokes blocked Chelsea Gray's reverse layup (ESPN play-by-play). The Golden State Valkyries won 83-81 and lead the Las Vegas Aces 2-0. Here's the wild part: Vegas led for 11 seconds all night, and this is a team that beat Golden State all three times in the regular season (ESPN game page). But what the Aces did in the fourth quarter is exactly why I'm not burying them yet.

The finish

Golden State was up 74-68 at 3:35 after Kalani Brown got hit with a defensive three seconds technical and Kayla Thornton hit the free throw. Then Vegas ripped off eight straight. Dana Evans hit a three at 2:43. Gray hit another at 2:10 to tie it at 74, and Jackie Young assisted both. A'ja Wilson drove for a layup at 1:36, and the Aces had their first lead of the night, 76-74 (play-by-play).

It lasted 11 seconds. Williams answered with a 13-foot pull-up at 1:25. Gray missed a 29-footer and Vegas got hit with a shot clock violation. Then Burton's dagger.

The last 41 seconds were a free throw contest. Stephanie Talbot blocked a Williams jumper at 14.3, and Gray made two at the line to make it 79-78. Thornton went 2-for-2. Young went 2-for-2. Burton went 2-for-2 for 83-80 at 6.4. Young made the first at 5.9 and missed the second, Gray grabbed the board, and Stokes met her at the rim. Burton got the rebound and that was it (play-by-play).

How Golden State built the lead

The first half was all Valkyries. They jumped out 17-7 in the first quarter and led 19-11 after one. In the second, Tiffany Hayes went off. She hit a 26-foot three at 9:20, a pull-up at 8:36, an and-one at 6:53 and another bucket at 5:50. That's 10 points in about three and a half minutes, and Golden State led 42-31 at the half (ESPN game page).

The lead hit 17 at 64-47 on two Thornton free throws late in the third. It was 66-55 going into the fourth. Golden State led 95 percent of the game (ESPN box score).

The numbers that matter

Hayes was the difference off the bench: 21 points in 17 minutes on 6-of-10 shooting and a perfect 8-of-8 at the line. Williams scored 20 but needed 17 shots. Thornton had 16 and went 7-of-7 at the line. Burton only scored 8, but 3 of them were the biggest of the night. Stokes didn't score at all, and she still grabbed 7 rebounds and made the play that sealed it (box score).

Here's what will drive Vegas crazy. The Aces outshot Golden State 44 percent to 41 percent and hit 42 percent of their threes to Golden State's 34. They had 19 assists to 12. They lost anyway. Golden State grabbed 10 offensive rebounds to Vegas's 6, went 25-of-27 at the line, and got 8 fast break points. Vegas got zero in transition (box score).

Wilson did her part with 24 points on 9-of-16 and 6-of-6 at the line. Gray had 19 and 6 assists. Young only made 2 of her 7 shots but scored 14 by going 8-of-10 at the line, with 8 assists and 4 steals. Talbot gave them 10 on 3-of-4 shooting in 21 minutes. Jewell Loyd didn't play and isn't in the box score (box score).

Game 3 take

The series heads to Las Vegas on Friday at 6:30 PM PT on Peacock and NBCSN (ESPN game page). Vegas scored 60 in Game 1 and dropped 26 in this fourth quarter, so the offense is waking up. The problem is the starts. You can't score 11 in a first quarter and spot a team this tough 17 points.

My call: the Aces win Game 3 at home. They shot better, passed better, and still had the ball with a chance to tie at the end. Golden State is the better rebounding team in this series, though, and if Vegas lets them win the glass again, this thing is over by the weekend.
