    WNBA 2026 Finals: Valkyries can clinch in Game 6, but road teams in this spot are 2-4

    By Ryan MoalemiOctober 27, 2026

    Follow the play-by-play: Valkyries at Dream, Game 6 →

    The Golden State Valkyries lead the Atlanta Dream 3-2 and can win the title in Game 6. Since 2002, road teams in their spot are 2-4.

    Road teams closing out a Finals, since 2002

    Since 2002, six road teams have led the Finals with a chance to clinch on the road after the home team had already won a game in the series. That is Golden State's spot on Thursday, and only two of those road teams won. Here are those six games:

    Since 2002, road teams leading the series with a chance to clinch are 8-8, not counting winner-take-all deciders. They are 2-4 when the home team had already won a game in the series, which is Golden State's spot Thursday. Golden State can win the title in Game 6 on Thursday at 9:30 a.m. PT (12:30 p.m. ET) at State Farm Arena in Atlanta, even though Atlanta has outscored it 357-335.

    All six games were decided by 7 points or fewer, and three by 2 or fewer. Three of the four road teams that lost the closeout won the title at home in the next game: Minnesota in 2015, Washington in 2019 and New York in 2024. The 2007 Detroit Shock lost Game 5 at home to Phoenix. Road teams whose opponent had not yet won a game went 6-4 in their closeout games; the CSV has all 16 games.

    Golden State's points per game would be the lowest for a champion since 2002

    Golden State is averaging 67.0 points per game in the Finals. If it wins Game 6 while scoring 65 or fewer, its 66.7 average would be the lowest for a champion since 2002, just under the 2005 Sacramento Monarchs' 66.75.     If the series goes seven, Golden State would need 132 or fewer combined points in Games 6 and 7 to finish under 66.75. Since 2002, only the 2006 Detroit Shock (outscored by 9) and the 2016 Los Angeles Sparks (by 5) have won three of the first five Finals games while being outscored; both won the title. Golden State is at minus-22.

    Keys to victory for Game 6

    Atlanta Dream

    Push the pace

    Atlanta's two wins were the two fastest games of the series, at about 82 and 83 possessions, and it scored 79 and 97. Its three losses ran about 80, 73 and 72 possessions, and it scored 64, 62 and 55. Speed has not tired out Stokes's shot blocking, though: she had 4 blocks in each of Games 1 and 2 and 3 in each of Games 3-5.

    Get Reese going

    Angel Reese scored 30 and 21 on 17 of 28 shooting in Atlanta's wins and 14, 8 and 7 on 9 of 31 in its losses.

    Score at the rim

    Atlanta scored 24 and 40 points on paint finishes in its wins and 22, 18 and 14 in its losses. Free throws have not decided games: Atlanta took 31 free throws to Golden State's 13 in Game 4 and still lost 67-62.

    Don't leave Stokes open

    Kiah Stokes missed all four of her 3-point tries in Games 1-4, then made 3 of 4 in Game 5 and scored 13, her high for the series, in a 9-point Atlanta loss.

    Win the turnover battle

    The team with fewer turnovers has won all five games. Atlanta had 14 and 8 in its wins, against Golden State's 18 and 15, and 12, 17 and 15 in its losses. The team with more points off turnovers has also won every game: Atlanta outscored Golden State 46-20 off turnovers in its two wins, and Golden State won that count 54-22 in its three.

    Try Reese at the 4

    Reese shot 22 of 39 (56 percent) for 65 points in Games 1-3 and 4 of 20 (20 percent) for 15 in Games 4-5. Court Queens LA (@courtqueens101) suggested on X that Smesko play Madina Okot at the 5 and Reese at the 4, so Stokes has to guard Okot. Okot has played 3:56 in the series, and the pair have shared the floor for under two minutes.

    Golden State Valkyries

    Hold Atlanta to 64 or fewer

    Golden State has won every game in which Atlanta scored 64 or fewer (64, 62 and 55) and lost both games in which Atlanta scored 79 or more.

    Take care of the ball

    Golden State turned it over 8, 9 and 7 times in its wins and 18 and 15 times in its losses.

    Win the first half

    The halftime leader has won all five games. Golden State led at the half by 3, 16 and 3 in its wins and trailed by 2 and 6 in its losses.

    Make it a half-court game

    Golden State's last two wins were the two slowest games of the series, about 73 and 72 possessions in Games 4 and 5, and Atlanta's paint scoring fell from 40 in Game 3 to 18 and 14. Its fast-break points fell from 19 and 13 in Games 2 and 3 to 2 in each of Games 4 and 5.

    Keep the Game 4 starting five

    Golden State started Kayla Thornton and Cecilia Zandalasini with Stokes, Gabby Williams and Burton in Games 1-3 and went 1-2. Natalie Nakase started Kaila Charles and Tiffany Hayes in their place in Games 4 and 5, and Golden State won both. The rotation got shorter too: seven Valkyries played at least five minutes in each of those games, down from nine, nine and 10 in Games 1-3, and Burton played about 38 and 37 minutes.

    Attendance record watch

    The 2026 Finals have drawn 88,390 fans through five games. A crowd of 4,210 on Thursday would pass the 2024 Finals record of 92,599, the most since 2002, and State Farm Arena seats more than 17,000. Our Finals attendance tracker has every game, and the data is free to reuse under CC0.

    Where the trophy would be raised

    If Golden State wins Thursday, it would celebrate on a court that has to be ready for an NBA game a few hours later. The Hawks host the Cleveland Cavaliers at State Farm Arena at 8 p.m. ET that night (NBA.com), with doors at 7 p.m. (State Farm Arena). That leaves seven and a half hours from Game 6 tip-off to the Hawks' tip, and six and a half hours to the Hawks' doors, before accounting for overtime or a trophy ceremony.

    ESPN's Ramona Shelburne reported on NBA Today on Oct. 26 that the celebration "may not actually be on that court." She said, "I've heard there are discussions about whether there's enough time to turn the arena over in time for the fans to celebrate on the court. It could be moved to like a practice court" (Yahoo Sports). She later posted on X: "To clarify: There is a resolve to make sure any potential championship that is won Thursday afternoon is celebrated on the court" (The Big Lead). The WNBA's Game 6 announcement does not mention a trophy plan (WNBA), and we found no Dream or arena statement on it as of Tuesday.

    Only Golden State can clinch Thursday. If Atlanta wins, Game 7 is Saturday, Oct. 31, at 5 p.m. PT (8 p.m. ET) at Chase Center in San Francisco (WNBA), and the trophy would be presented there.

    A second-year champion

    Golden State played its first season in 2025, when it became the first expansion team to make the playoffs in its first year (Valkyries). A title Thursday would come in its second season. The fastest expansion champion so far is the Seattle Storm, which joined in 2000 and won in 2004, its fifth season (WNBA). Neither finalist has won a WNBA title, so 2026 will crown a first-time champion (WNBA).

    Why it's at 9:30 a.m. PT

    Game 6 was originally set for 8 p.m. ET. The WNBA moved it to 12:30 p.m. ET at State Farm Arena, which seats more than 17,000, instead of the Dream's usual home, Gateway Center Arena (about 3,500). The Hawks agreed to move their game against Cleveland that night to 8 p.m. ET (WNBA). "The sellout crowds and extraordinary atmosphere at State Farm Arena for Games 3 and 4 were a tremendous success, and we're excited to give thousands more fans the opportunity to experience Game 6 with a championship on the line," Commissioner Cathy Engelbert said in the announcement.

    Atlanta coach Karl Smesko said he was excited Atlanta could pack a big arena and joked, "I'd like to see it be a national holiday for all WNBA championship games" (Yahoo Sports). He also said there would be no pregame shootaround for either team (Yahoo Sports). The weekday midday tip drew the loudest reaction on X and r/wnba this week (Deadline).

    Dream ownership answered in an open letter to fans on Tuesday. "Two months ago, when the WNBA finalized playoff dates and times, we raised concerns that the W Finals schedule conflicted with NBA games at State Farm Arena," it said, adding, "Playing a midday game on a Thursday is not what we would have chosen and not what we fought for" (CBS Sports). The Dream announced on Oct. 6 that it will play all of its home games at State Farm Arena starting in 2027 (Atlanta Dream).

    Four things to watch

    The third quarter. Atlanta has won the third quarter 100-68 across five games. Golden State has won the other three quarters 267-257. The team that led at halftime has won all five games, and so has the team with fewer turnovers (Game 5 recap).

    Reese against Stokes. Reese averaged 21.7 points in Games 1-3 and 7.5 in Games 4-5. Stokes called Reese "the head of the snake" after holding her to 2 of 10 shooting in Game 4 (USA Today). Stokes has at least 3 blocks in every game of the series, and her 17 blocks are a Finals series record, passing Sylvia Fowles and Brittney Griner (WNBA on X).

    Atlanta's Game 5 hole. The Dream shot 19 of 65 (29.2 percent), turned it over 15 times and got 4 points from the bench (WNBA.com box score). "That's going to be a very ruthless Atlanta team that really wants it just as much. You can never count them out," Golden State coach Natalie Nakase said after Game 5 (SI).

    Free throws. Atlanta averaged 29.0 free-throw attempts in Games 1-4 and took 14 in Game 5. "The contact they allow on Angel compared to everybody else is just ridiculous," Smesko said after Game 5 (SI). Reese said, "It definitely shifted when we went up 2-1" (same source).

    Finals MVP race

    If Golden State wins, the Finals MVP debate is between Williams, its leading scorer, and Stokes, whose case rests on defense. Kalshi's Finals MVP market had Williams at 57 percent, Reese 27 and Stokes 18 on Tuesday (Kalshi). CBS Sports ranks the field after Game 5 (CBS Sports).
