Clark Hits Back-to-Back Threes, Fever Even Series with Aces

By Ryan Moalemi

Fever 99, Aces 89. Clark hits two threes in 26 seconds and Indiana ties the series.

With 4:56 left and the Aces finally within striking distance, Caitlin Clark rose up from 27 feet. Aliyah Boston found her. Splash. Then, after Jackie Young knocked down two free throws, Boston found her again. Clark stepped back from 30. Another three. In 26 seconds Gainbridge went from nervous to loud again, and an 84-77 cushion became 90-79. That stretch is why Indiana walks out of Game 2 with a 99-89 win and a series tied 1-1.

Las Vegas had every reason to believe. The Aces opened hotter, up 26-18 after one and by as many as 11 in the second. Jackie Young was cooking. A'ja Wilson was living at the line. Then the Fever flipped the game inside. Indiana scored 58 points in the paint to Las Vegas's 26. Boston finished 10-of-15 for 22 points and five assists. Clark piled on 27 points, seven boards, and 15 assists. Kelsey Mitchell added 26.

The second quarter told you where this was headed. Down 33-22, Indiana went on a tear. Clark scored and scored again. Mitchell knocked down a three. Raven Johnson and Boston kept finishing at the rim. Suddenly it was 46-39 Fever, and they took a 48-43 lead into the break. The third was more of the same. Clark hit a 31-footer with under two minutes left. Mitchell drained a three in the final seconds. Indiana led 72-60 after three and never really looked back, even when Las Vegas tried to.

That Aces push in the fourth was real. Young hit a three. Jewell Loyd hit another. Free throws kept coming. In a few minutes a 14-point hole was down to three, 76-73. Gainbridge got quiet. Then Boston drove for a layup, Wilson answered, and Clark ended the argument with those two threes. Boston kept finishing layups down the stretch. Mitchell and Sophie Cunningham made their free throws. Young led everybody with 31, and Wilson had 22 and nine, but after her 38-point night in Game 1, Indiana never let this one get away.

Game 3 decides it. Indiana just proved it can punch the paint and survive a Las Vegas run. The Aces still have Young cooking and Wilson getting to the line. Whoever wins the next one advances. No middle ground left.
