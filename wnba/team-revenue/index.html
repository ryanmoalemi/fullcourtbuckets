    WNBA team revenue and franchise value multiples

    By Ryan Moalemi|Updated Oct. 31, 2026

    Game 7 of the 2026 WNBA Finals pairs Playbook Strategies' highest 2025 revenue estimate with one of its two lowest. The Golden State Valkyries are at $78 million. The Atlanta Dream are at $16 million, tied with the Washington Mystics. Golden State's estimate is 4.875 times Atlanta's, nearly 5 times. Using Forbes' May 7, 2026 values, Atlanta's franchise value is 15.6 times that Playbook revenue and Golden State's is 10.0 times.

    Those FCB multiples use one valuation column, the Forbes cards, and one revenue column, the Oct. 31, 2026 Playbook graphic posted by Katherine Rowe. They match the graphic for 11 of 13 teams. They do not match for Indiana (FCB 10.4x, graphic 10.0x) or Washington (FCB 17.5x, graphic 15.6x). Playbook's revenue for those two teams differs from the revenue Forbes printed, and the graphic's multiples match the Forbes revenue.

    Where the gap is

    With 13 teams, the closest whole count to a quartile is three. The top three by Playbook revenue are Golden State Valkyries, Indiana Fever, New York Liberty. They combine for $177 million of the $426 million Playbook total, 41.5 percent. The bottom three are Connecticut Sun, Washington Mystics, Atlanta Dream, combining for $52 million, 12.2 percent. The top group is 3.4 times the bottom group.

    Game 7 is the cleanest single gap. Golden State at $78 million against Atlanta at $16 million is 4.875 times, nearly 5 times, and a $62 million difference. The Forbes values are $780 million and $250 million, 3.12 times, a $530 million difference. Washington shares Atlanta's $16 million Playbook revenue but not its value. Washington is $280 million, so the FCB multiple is 17.5x, above Atlanta's 15.6x. On the Forbes revenue column Washington is $18 million, and Atlanta is the only team at $16 million.

    Reading the multiple

    This section is FCB analysis of the spread. It is not a Forbes or Playbook finding about a named team.

    A 15.6x club has not been shown to be a better business than a 10.0x club. The multiple says the May 2026 valuation is that many times the 2025 revenue estimate. Forbes applied about 10 times revenue to Golden State and about 12.5 times average revenue to the league. The article said bankers and investors were cautious about paying a premium for a franchise whose revenue is new, even while calling that revenue real.

    Lower-revenue teams can still print higher multiples when a buyer is pricing growth, scarcity, or a building the club does not fully use yet. Atlanta's home arena on the WNBA.com listing is Gateway Center Arena. Golden State plays at Chase Center. The revenue gap and the multiple gap can describe the same building constraint from opposite sides: a small revenue base, and a price that assumes the base will not stay that small. Connecticut is the sale closest to these cards. Forbes reported an agreement for about $300 million, the same number as the Sun's Forbes value, with plans to move the team to Houston.

    Sportico's separate May 2026 model is not in the value column. It puts Golden State at $850 million, the league average at $427 million, and the 13 teams at $5.55 billion including real estate. Sportico's league revenue estimate for those clubs is $410 million, against $426 million on the Playbook cards and $430 million on the Forbes cards. CNBC's $1 billion Golden State value uses a 15-team set. None of those replace the Forbes cards in this file.

    Methods

    2025 team revenue estimates in millions of U.S. dollars. The column 2025_revenue_usd_m is the Playbook Strategies figure used for the FCB multiple. Playoffs are excluded, as labeled on the Oct. 31, 2026 Playbook graphic and in the Forbes methodology. These are estimates, not audited financials.

    Forbes franchise value in millions of U.S. dollars from the May 7, 2026 ranking of the 13 teams that played in 2025. Forbes includes arena economics and excludes arena real estate. Values were read from the Forbes Mexico translation of the Brett Knight article, which prints the 13 team cards. The English Forbes URL is the canonical citation.

    value_to_revenue_multiple is a Full Court Buckets calculation: forbes_2026_value_usd_m divided by 2025_revenue_usd_m (the Playbook estimate), rounded half up to one decimal. It is not a multiple Forbes published team by team, except that Forbes stated Golden State at about 10 times 2025 revenue and the league at 12.5 times average 2025 regular-season revenue.

    Revenue is for the 2025 season. Forbes values are as of the May 7, 2026 ranking. This page was updated Oct. 31, 2026, during the 2026 WNBA Finals. The 2026 season's revenue is not in these figures.

    The English Forbes article did not return the team table to a client without JavaScript on Oct. 31, 2026. Every value, Forbes revenue, one-year change, and owner line was read from the Forbes Mexico translation of the same May 7, 2026 article, which prints the 13 cards and says the text was first published by Forbes US. Grey Journal's Oct. 23, 2026 table matches those cards, including a complete Atlanta row at $250 million, $16 million, and a 32 percent one-year change. Grey Journal conflicts with Playbook on Fever revenue ($58 million vs $56 million) and Mystics revenue ($18 million vs $16 million). It does not conflict with the Forbes prose that names the Fever at $58 million.

    Seattle's graphic multiple is recorded as 15.4x. That is the bar on the Oct. 31, 2026 graphic, and it is also $430 million divided by $28 million. Phoenix and Minnesota print at 15.2x. A short grouping of the same post put Seattle with the 15.2x clubs. This file keeps 15.4x.

    Arena names are the strings on WNBA.com team pages when FCB checked team facts on Oct. 28, 2026. They are context, not an input to the Forbes value.

    FAQ

    Which WNBA team had the highest 2025 revenue?

    Playbook Strategies estimates the Golden State Valkyries at $78 million for 2025, excluding playoffs. Forbes prints the same $78 million and calls it a WNBA record.

    Which WNBA teams had the lowest 2025 revenue?

    On the Playbook graphic, the Atlanta Dream and the Washington Mystics are tied at $16 million, the lowest estimate. Forbes prints Atlanta at $16 million and Washington at $18 million, so Atlanta is alone at the bottom of the Forbes revenue column.

    What is the franchise value multiple on this page?

    The FCB multiple is the May 7, 2026 Forbes value divided by the Playbook 2025 revenue estimate, rounded half up to one decimal. Atlanta is 15.6x. Golden State is 10.0x. Washington is 17.5x on that formula. The Playbook graphic prints Washington at 15.6x, which matches Forbes value divided by Forbes revenue ($280 million / $18 million), not by the $16 million in the same graphic.

    Why can a lower-revenue team show a higher multiple?

    A higher multiple means the valuation is a larger number of times last season's estimated revenue. It does not mean the business is stronger. Forbes valued the league at 12.5 times average 2025 regular-season revenue and Golden State at about 10 times, and wrote that some bankers were cautious about paying a premium for a new revenue base. Buyers can still price a lower-revenue club for growth, a scarce seat, or a move into a larger building. That reading is FCB analysis of the spread, not a claim Forbes or Playbook made about Atlanta or Washington.

    Do Forbes and Playbook use the same 2025 revenue?

    For 11 of the 13 teams, yes. They differ for the Indiana Fever (Playbook $56 million, Forbes $58 million) and the Washington Mystics (Playbook $16 million, Forbes $18 million). The graphic's 10.0x for Indiana and 15.6x for Washington match the Forbes revenue, not the Playbook revenue printed in the same row.

    Are the Portland Fire and Toronto Tempo in this dataset?

    No. Forbes left both out of the May 7, 2026 ranking because they begin play in the 2026 season. The Playbook graphic is the 13 teams that played in 2025. Cleveland, Detroit, and Philadelphia, which Forbes said begin play in 2028, 2029, and 2030, are out too. A FOX59 table that lists Portland and Toronto, and Golden State at $1 billion, does not match the Forbes cards and is not used.

    How current are these figures?

    Revenue is a 2025 season estimate. Forbes values are from the ranking dated May 7, 2026. This page was updated Oct. 31, 2026, during the WNBA Finals. It does not include 2026 season revenue.
